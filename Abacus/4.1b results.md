# National Drug and Health Products Authority Bill, 2025

## Approach

I treated "new" as:

- new versus the current National Drug Policy and Authority Act
- but not "truly new" if it is already standard under existing NDA regulations or normal GMP/GDP/pharmacovigilance practice for serious operators

I also expanded the sector model slightly because the bill is not only about supply-chain steps.

categories:

- `0: Cross-cutting / governance / enforcement`
- `1: R&D`
- `2: Licensing`
- `3: API sourcing`
- `4: Manufacture`
- `5: Packaging`
- `6: Logistics`
- `7: Wholesale`
- `8: Dispensing`
- `9: QA / surveillance / post-market`

🔴 New, and it has significant cost and pressure implicaitons on the existing large and ethical players
🟡 New, and the industry needs to make changes, but it's not dramatic
🟢 New, but the industry already does this. It exists to manage and align the unethical players

## Proposed New Regulations

| RAG |      Sector | Change                                                                                                                                                                                    | Implications                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --- | ----------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴  | 0,2,4,6,7,9 | Full statutory expansion beyond drugs into medical devices, cosmetics, public health products and nutritional supplements                                                                 | This is the biggest change. Large operators in these categories move into a full NDA-style regime with registration/listing/notification, licensing, inspections, sanctions and ongoing surveillance. For ethical players already working to international standards it is manageable, but it still adds real cost, approval friction and regulatory exposure. |
| 🔴  |     2,4,6,7 | Mandatory registration/listing/notification for medical devices, cosmetics, public health products and nutritional supplements                                                            | Many businesses that may previously have sold under lighter or fragmented oversight now need formal product-market authorisation. Portfolio rationalisation, dossier prep, local reps, renewal cycles and launch delays become much more likely.                                                                                                               |
| 🔴  |     2,4,6,7 | Mandatory licensing for manufacture, import, distribution and export of those non-drug categories                                                                                         | This turns several categories into fully licensed regulated trades. Existing ethical players can adapt, but the compliance burden becomes much heavier, especially for import-heavy distributors and brand owners.                                                                                                                                             |
| 🔴  |     9,5,6,7 | Product monitoring systems plus possible unique identifier on each regulated product package                                                                                              | This is a serious compliance step. It points toward traceability/serialization-style obligations, complaints handling and post-market monitoring. Large formal players can build this; smaller informal operators will struggle.                                                                                                                               |
| 🔴  |         4,9 | GMP, GSP and GDP certificates explicitly required across regulated product classes                                                                                                        | Ethical pharma players already do this for drugs, but the bill extends and hardens the expectation across devices, cosmetics, public health products and supplements. That is a material compliance cost for manufacturers and distributors in those sectors.                                                                                                  |
| 🔴  |       3,4,6 | Public health products categorised into listed/notified/registered/prohibited classes, including named prohibited actives such as DDT, disulfoton, chlordane, dieldrin and neonicotinoids | This creates hard market access restrictions and can force reformulation, supplier changes or portfolio exits. It is especially material for pesticide/public-health chemical suppliers.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 🔴  |         4,9 | Lot release certificates for vaccines, biologicals and diagnostics before market release                                                                                                  | This adds batch-level gatekeeping. For vaccine/biological/diagnostic manufacturers and exporters it creates direct release delays, testing dependence and working-capital pressure.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 🟡  |         7,8 | Drug distribution is expressly licensed as a statutory activity                                                                                                                           | The memorandum itself says the current Act does not regulate drug distribution well. Ethical distributors may already behave as if licensed under NDA rules, but this strengthens the legal basis and raises enforcement risk.                                                                                                                                 |
| 🟡  |         8,2 | Pharmacy licensing tightened, including corporate structure and pharmacist-role requirements, plus explicit coverage of pharmacies within private hospitals/clinics                       | This formalises ownership/supervision expectations and closes gaps around facility-based pharmacies. Large ethical chains and hospitals can comply, but structures and licence holders may need adjustment.                                                                                                                                                    |
| 🟡  |         9,0 | NDA laboratory formally established, with test reports usable as prima facie evidence                                                                                                     | This strengthens enforcement considerably. It is not a big operational burden day one, but it increases the state's ability to prosecute, detain and challenge products.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 🟡  |         0,9 | Inspectors gain clearer powers to seize products, refuse entry, close and seal premises, and trigger destruction orders                                                                   | Ethical players should usually avoid these powers being used against them, but the bill sharply increases enforcement leverage and operational disruption risk when issues arise.                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 🟡  |     6,7,8,9 | Mandatory record-keeping of supply for regulated products                                                                                                                                 | Most serious firms already keep batch and distribution records, so this is not conceptually new, but it broadens the obligation and makes it enforceable across more product classes and actors.                                                                                                                                                               |
| 🟡  |           6 | Approved ports of entry/exit for all regulated products                                                                                                                                   | This can affect logistics design, customs planning and landed cost. Large compliant importers can adapt, but it may reduce routing flexibility.                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 🟡  |           9 | Mandatory recall/withdrawal powers extended clearly across drugs and all regulated products                                                                                               | Ethical firms already run recall processes, but the bill gives the Authority stronger statutory footing to order recalls, destruction and suspension of supply.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 🟡  |         1,9 | Clinical trials/field trials extended beyond drugs to devices, cosmetics, supplements and public health products                                                                          | R&D and market-entry teams for these categories face a more formal authorisation regime. This is especially relevant for innovative devices and efficacy/safety claims.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| 🟡  |         3,4 | Minister may prohibit importation of cosmetic products posing human risk or containing unjustified risky ingredients                                                                      | This gives government a sharp intervention tool against certain cosmetic formulations. Large brands may need ingredient review and regulatory contingency planning.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 🟡  |         6,7 | Donation imports of drugs require NDA authorisation, conditions and shelf-life approval, with ministerial approval for government donations                                               | This tightens what is often an abuse channel. Legitimate donors can comply, but emergency and humanitarian supply chains become more procedural.                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 🟢  |           9 | Pharmacovigilance systems for drug manufacturers/importers/distributors are put directly into the Act                                                                                     | This is new at primary Act level, but not new in practice for serious operators because Uganda already has pharmacovigilance regulations and ethical firms already run PV systems. It mainly tightens the position against weaker players.                                                                                                                     |
| 🟢  |         1,9 | Good clinical practice and adverse event reporting duties for drug trials are put directly into the Act                                                                                   | Again, this mostly codifies what serious sponsors and CROs already do under existing regulation and international norms. It primarily strengthens enforcement against poor-quality trial activity.                                                                                                                                                             |
| 🟢  |       5,7,8 | Packaging, labelling, anti-falsification, substandard-product and misleading-advertisement controls are extended and consolidated                                                         | Ethical players already work within these norms. The real effect is stronger action against counterfeiters, grey importers and low-discipline traders.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| 🟢  |           4 | Local research and production encouragement, including herbal and other medical products                                                                                                  | Strategically important, but not a heavy compliance burden. It is more an industrial policy signal than a direct cost imposition.                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 🟢  |           8 | Power to make regulations for electronic pharmacy requests without physical appearance                                                                                                    | This signals future e-pharmacy rules rather than imposing them immediately. Ethical players are likely already preparing for this direction.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

## What Looks Genuinely New vs Mostly Codified

- Genuinely new / materially expanded: statutory coverage of medical devices, cosmetics, public health products and nutritional supplements; lot release; public-health-product categorisation and prohibited substances; clear powers to seal premises; product traceability/unique identifiers; full licensing stack for newly covered categories.
- Mostly codified or elevated from current practice: pharmacovigilance, clinical-trial controls, import/export licensing logic for drugs, advertising controls, GMP/GDP expectations, recalls, records, premises inspection.

## Bottom Line

The bill is not just a cleanup. It is a major market expansion of Uganda's health-product regulator from a mainly drug-focused regime into a broad product regulator.

The biggest commercial effect is on:

- device importers and distributors
- cosmetic brands/importers
- pesticide/public-health product suppliers
- supplement companies
- vaccine/biological/diagnostic manufacturers
