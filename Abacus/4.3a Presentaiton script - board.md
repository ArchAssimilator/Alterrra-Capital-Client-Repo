

# Board deck prompt

## Role

You are an experienced strategy and regulatory communications advisor preparing executive-level presentation content for board members. You know how to turn detailed regulatory analysis into concise, decision-useful slide copy for senior business audiences. You write for clarity, brevity, and commercial relevance, not for legal or technical specialists.

## Context

The source material is an existing analysis of a proposed Act that has already been reviewed and classified using a Red, Yellow, Green framework:

* Red: genuinely new and materially important changes
* Yellow: changes that require adjustment but are less significant
* Green: changes that are largely codified existing practice

The next step is to convert that analysis into a slide script for use in Gamma.app.

The audience is a board audience. They need:

* a quick explanation of what the Act does
* a clear explanation of the Red, Yellow, Green framework and why items were classified that way
* focused discussion of the Red items only
* clear business implications, especially operational, strategic, and cost implications

Gamma slides have limited screen space, so the content must be concise and presentation-ready.

## Task

Create a slide script in markdown that can be pasted directly into Gamma.app.

You should:

* decide the most effective slide structure yourself
* include an opening section that explains the Act and summarises the Red, Yellow, Green logic
* decide whether that opening section works best as one slide or multiple slides
* focus the rest of the deck on the Red items only
* decide whether each Red item should have its own slide or whether some should be grouped, based on what best fits the screen space and board audience needs
* explain the business implications of each Red item
* explicitly highlight likely cost implications, even where they cannot be quantified precisely

## Instructions

* Write for board members: commercial, high-level, clear, and concise
* Prioritise judgment and usability over completeness
* Keep each slide tight enough for Gamma.app screen space
* Do not overload slides with legal detail
* Focus on what matters for decision-makers: risk, cost, operational burden, strategic significance, and business exposure
* Use only the Red items for the main body of the deck
* You may group related Red items where this improves readability and flow
* Include image prompts where useful, but keep them optional and simple
* Output markdown only

## Output

Produce the slide script in this format for each slide:

* `Title:`
* `Subtitle:`
* `Content:`
* `Image prompt:` (optional)

Separate each slide clearly using markdown slide dividers, for example:

# Slide 1

Title: National Drug and Health Products Authority Bill, 2025
Subtitle: Board view of the most material regulatory changes for a large ethical health-products business
Content:

- This Bill is not just an update to drug regulation: it materially broadens the regulatory perimeter and strengthens enforcement powers.
- For established companies, the biggest issue is not routine GMP-type compliance, it is the expansion of scope, new hard-stop regulatory controls, and higher operational disruption risk.
- Our assessment separates changes into:
  - Red: genuinely new and likely to create meaningful cost, process, or market-access pressure
  - Yellow: requires adjustment, but is manageable and often aligns with existing good practice
  - Green: mostly codifies what ethical operators already do, mainly raising pressure on weaker players
    Image prompt: A clean boardroom-style regulatory dashboard with red, yellow, and green indicators over a pharmaceutical and health-products supply chain map

# Slide 2

Title: Why we have classified the Bill this way
Subtitle: The judgment is about practical newness, not just whether wording is new in law
Content:

- We treated provisions as Red where the Bill clearly creates new legal powers, extends regulation into previously uncovered product classes, or gives the Authority tools that can directly interrupt operations.
- We treated provisions as Yellow where the Bill requires change, but largely builds on controls that mature regulated companies already operate in practice.
- We treated provisions as Green where the Bill mainly codifies standard ethical-industry behaviour, especially around quality, documentation, and product integrity.
- The Bill itself says the current law does not adequately cover distribution, pharmacies in health units, lot release, recall and withdrawal, laboratory testing, and seal-off powers. Those are strong indicators of genuine change.
  Image prompt: A simple executive framework showing three tiers: genuinely new and disruptive, new but manageable, and codified existing practice

---

# Slide 3

Title: Executive summary of the RAG picture
Subtitle: Most of the board-level risk sits in a relatively small number of Red items
Content:

- Red: a few structural changes with disproportionate impact on market access, compliance burden, and business interruption risk
- Yellow: broader operational uplift, especially in post-market systems, product monitoring, traceability, and import-distribution controls
- Green: important, but mainly reinforces expectations already met by large ethical operators
- Bottom line: the real board issue is the combination of wider product scope plus stronger regulator intervention powers
  Image prompt: Executive infographic showing a few large red blocks, several medium yellow blocks, and smaller green blocks, styled for a healthcare company board pack

# Slide 4

Title: Red item 1, expansion of the regulated perimeter
Subtitle: The Authority moves beyond drugs into devices, certain cosmetics, public health products, and nutritional supplements
Content:

- This is the single biggest structural change in the Bill.
- Businesses active in adjacent portfolios may move from lighter or fragmented oversight into a full registration, licensing, inspection, and enforcement regime.
- Product lines that may previously have been treated as commercial or consumer-health adjacencies may now need full regulatory ownership.
- Board implication: this can change which parts of the portfolio are strategically attractive, and which now carry heavier compliance overhead.
- Cost implications:
  - new registrations, notifications, and listings
  - new licence applications and renewals
  - additional regulatory staffing and local responsible-person coverage
  - potential portfolio pruning where low-margin lines no longer justify compliance cost
    Image prompt: A pharmaceutical portfolio expanding outward into medical devices, cosmetics, supplements, and public health products, with regulatory boundaries tightening around them

---

# Slide 5

Title: Red item 2, new market-entry controls across newly regulated categories
Subtitle: Registration and licensing become a formal gate to manufacture, import, export, distribution, and supply
Content:

- For newly covered product classes, this creates a much harder legal gate to entering and staying in the market.
- Even for established companies, this means more structured dossier management, licence dependency, renewal tracking, and exposure to delay.
- The issue is not only compliance cost, it is time-to-market and continuity of supply.
- Board implication: market access becomes more regulator-dependent across a wider set of products.
- Cost implications:
  - submission and maintenance fees
  - dossier preparation and remediation work
  - longer launch timelines and tied-up working capital
  - higher risk of stranded stock if approvals or renewals slip
    Image prompt: A market access funnel with regulatory checkpoints for registration, import, distribution, and supply of health products

---

# Slide 6

Title: Red item 3, explicit recall, withdrawal, and destruction powers
Subtitle: The Bill gives the Authority clearer legal tools to remove products from the market
Content:

- The Bill appears to create a much clearer statutory recall and withdrawal regime than the current law.
- Ethical companies already run recall processes, but this change raises the legal force, speed, and consequences of regulator action.
- The real business issue is execution pressure: traceability, batch visibility, communications, stock segregation, and decision-making must work fast.
- Board implication: quality events become more likely to turn into immediate revenue loss, supply disruption, and reputational damage.
- Cost implications:
  - product retrieval, destruction, replacement, and logistics
  - customer, pharmacy, and provider communication costs
  - increased inventory write-offs
  - heightened brand and tender risk after any visible field action
    Image prompt: A product recall scenario with supply chain nodes lighting up from manufacturer to warehouse to pharmacy, styled as a clean executive visual

---

# Slide 7

Title: Red item 4, stronger inspection and site-intervention powers
Subtitle: Inspectors can seize product, refuse entry, and close or seal premises
Content:

- This is a major enforcement escalation.
- The Bill gives the Authority tools that can directly interrupt operations rather than only punish after the fact.
- For good operators, the biggest risk is not deliberate misconduct, it is a serious quality, documentation, or licensing gap discovered at the wrong time.
- Board implication: regulatory non-compliance can now become an immediate operational shutdown issue.
- Cost implications:
  - halted production or distribution activity
  - delayed imports and stock shortages
  - remediation programmes at site level
  - legal, advisory, and crisis-management spend
    Image prompt: A compliant pharmaceutical facility facing a regulatory stop action at the gate, shown in a sober executive style

---

# Slide 8

Title: Red item 5, lot release for vaccines, biologicals, and diagnostics
Subtitle: Additional release control before products can enter the market
Content:

- This appears genuinely new and is operationally important for higher-risk biologic and diagnostic categories.
- It inserts an additional regulatory step into release planning and may affect speed, inventory, and launch timing.
- For companies in vaccines, biologics, or some diagnostics, this is a real supply-chain and working-capital issue.
- Board implication: more regulatory friction in release can weaken agility, especially in tender, outbreak, or time-sensitive supply situations.
- Cost implications:
  - longer batch release timelines
  - added holding costs and inventory exposure
  - greater forecasting and cold-chain planning burden
  - higher risk of missed delivery commitments
    Image prompt: Vaccine and biologic batches moving through an extra regulatory release gate before entering the market

# Slide 9

Title: Red item 6, clearer regulation of pharmacies in health units and drug distribution
Subtitle: More formal control of wholesale, retail, and embedded pharmacy operations
Content:

- The Bill addresses areas the current law reportedly does not adequately cover, especially drug distribution and pharmacies in health units.
- For hospital groups, private provider networks, and pharmacy-facing businesses, this is a direct operating model issue.
- It also tightens the legal basis for pharmacist accountability and premises licensing in the distribution chain.
- Board implication: distribution and dispensing channels become more tightly regulated, with less tolerance for inconsistent entity structures or local workarounds.
- Cost implications:
  - licence remediation across sites and entities
  - pharmacist coverage and supervision costs
  - premises upgrade and inspection-readiness spend
  - possible restructuring of hospital, clinic, and retail pharmacy arrangements
    Image prompt: A healthcare distribution network linking wholesaler, hospital pharmacy, retail pharmacy, and clinic, overlaid with licensing and supervision markers

# Slide 10

Title: What the Red items mean for the business
Subtitle: The pressure is operational, financial, and strategic
Content:

- Operationally: more approvals, more inspections, more traceability demands, and greater exposure to hard-stop interventions
- Financially: higher ongoing compliance cost, more launch friction, more risk of stock write-offs, and higher cost of remediation when issues arise
- Strategically: some product lines may become less attractive, while scale, quality maturity, and formal systems become a bigger competitive advantage
- Sector effect: this Bill is likely to hurt weaker informal or semi-compliant operators more than large ethical companies, but it still raises the cost base and governance burden for everyone
  Image prompt: A board-level strategy slide showing operations, finance, and strategy impacts radiating from a central regulatory reform theme

# Slide 11

Title: Board questions this Bill should trigger now
Subtitle: Decisions needed before the law is finalised and before implementation begins
Content:

- Which current or planned portfolio lines would fall into newly regulated categories?
- Where do we have the biggest exposure to recall, lot release, or import-stop disruption?
- Are our distribution entities, pharmacy-facing arrangements, and site licences structured to withstand tighter inspection?
- Which non-drug portfolios would need uplift to pharmaceutical-grade regulatory and quality control?
- What level of central programme management do we need so implementation is not fragmented across functions?
  Image prompt: A boardroom table with a strategic checklist on regulatory readiness, portfolio exposure, and implementation governance

# Slide 12

Title: Bottom line for the board
Subtitle: The Bill strengthens the hand of the regulator and raises the cost of operating at scale
Content:

- The headline issue is not routine compliance codification, it is the combination of wider regulatory scope and sharper intervention powers.
- The companies best placed to win will be those with disciplined regulatory, quality, and supply-chain systems across the whole portfolio, not just medicines.
- Our recommendation is to treat this as a strategic readiness programme, not a narrow legal review.
  Image prompt: A confident healthcare leadership team reviewing a regulatory transformation roadmap with portfolio, quality, and supply chain themes
