
A pharmaceutical company is evaluating the launch of a single-dose maternal RSV vaccine in Uganda. The vaccine would be given to pregnant women in the third trimester through antenatal-care channels to protect infants in the first months of life.

Uganda is an attractive but constrained market-entry opportunity. The country has a large birth cohort and meaningful unmet need, but commercial success will depend less on clinical demand alone and more on funded access, ANC reach, provider recommendation, patient acceptance, and cold-chain execution.

The opportunity should be assessed across four regions:

- Central
- Western
- Eastern
- Northern

Central is expected to be the strongest commercial region because it has the best provider access, the greatest private-market potential, the highest ability to pay, and the lowest distribution complexity. Western is the next most attractive. Eastern offers scale but lower affordability. Northern has high need but weaker affordability and more difficult logistics.

The business would likely use a mixed model. Most volume would come from public and donor-supported channels at lower realised prices. Private hospitals and maternity clinics would contribute smaller volume but higher prices.

Product would be imported and distributed through a cold-chain network to facilities. Distribution costs should be lowest in Central, moderate in Western, higher in Eastern, and highest in Northern. The company would also need a lean level of regulatory, market-access, medical-education, launch, and country-overhead support.

Over a 36-month period, the market should ramp gradually, with Central leading, Western following, and Eastern and Northern scaling more slowly. The core question is whether Uganda can become a commercially viable maternal RSV market with disciplined launch costs and a mix of public access and selective private monetization.
