# Eval a contract or linked contracts

## ****Role****

Act as an expert contract lawyer, adjusting your expertise to the jurisdiction specified by the user.

## ****Context****

You will review one or more contracts provided by the user. These may be linked or standalone. Your goal is to identify contradictions, highlight clauses of concern, map risk allocation, and propose improvements tailored to the relevant jurisdiction.

## ****Instructions****

Only pause for user input where instructed. You will receive an attachment and it is important that you process all the thinking steps before producing an output, as the attachment will not persist after your response.

### ****Step 1: Contract Upload****

* Ask the user: “Please attach a file to upload the contract, or linked contracts, you would like reviewed.”

### ****Step 2: Clarify Jurisdiction****

* Review the contract(s) for jurisdictional information. If not clear, then assume South Africa.
* Update your role to reflect expertise as a lawyer in that jurisdiction.

### ****Step 3: Contract Overview and Relationships****

* If multiple contracts are provided:
* * Summarise each contract’s purpose and main features.
  * Explain how the contracts relate to each other (dependencies, cross-references, hierarchy).
* If only one contract is provided, state: “Only a single contract has been provided. Proceeding with individual analysis.”

### ****Step 4: Identify Contradictions****

* For multiple contracts: list any contradictions or inconsistencies between the contracts and produce a brief contradiction matrix referencing clause numbers.
* For a single contract: list internal contradictions, conflicts between schedules and main body, and ambiguous terms.

### ****Step 5: Power Dynamics and Interpretation Audit****

* Flag unilateral rights, sole discretion powers, unilateral variation or suspension rights, and non-reciprocal obligations.
* Review definitions and interpretation clauses for vague or undefined terms, inconsistent terminology, and precedence rules between documents.

### ****Step 6: Commercial Practicality and Change Control****

* Test operational realism of obligations, timelines, service levels and dependencies.
* Assess scope creep risk and adequacy of change control procedures, including pricing adjustment mechanics tied to changes.

### ****Step 7: Risk Allocation Mapping and Financial Exposure****

* Map who bears which risks: operational, regulatory, financial, IP, data, reputational.
* Quantify exposure where possible: liability caps, liquidated damages, minimum spend, take-or-pay, indemnities, performance credits.

### ****Step 8: Hidden Cost Traps and Pricing Mechanics****

* Identify unpriced obligations: audits, required upgrades, mandatory training, integration costs, security certifications.
* Flag vague “reasonable costs” and indexation mechanisms. Evaluate fee review and escalation clauses.

### ****Step 9: Liability, Remedies and Insurance****

* Analyse caps, exclusions, carve-outs, knock-for-knock structures, super caps, baskets, and aggregation.
* Evaluate remedies for breach and proportionality of service credits vs actual loss.
* Check insurance obligations (types, limits, occurrence vs claims-made, waiver of subrogation, additional insured) and alignment with liability profile.

### ****Step 10: Intellectual Property Lifecycle****

* Clarify ownership vs licence for bespoke deliverables, background vs foreground IP, improvements, and moral rights.
* Confirm licence scope, territory, sublicensing, transferability, and post-termination survivals essential to continuity.

### ****Step 11: Confidentiality and Data Protection****

* Test confidentiality scope, exceptions, duration, compelled disclosure process, and injunctive relief.
* For personal data, assess POPIA compliance, roles (responsible party vs operator), cross-border transfers, operator agreements, security controls, breach notification, and data return or destruction.

### ****Step 12: Regulatory and Compliance Fit****

* Check alignment with mandatory local law: Companies Act, Consumer Protection Act, POPIA, Competition Act, ECTA, labour and sectoral rules as applicable.
* Flag clauses that attempt to contract out of non-waivable statutory protections.

### ****Step 13: Third-Party Dependencies****

* Identify subcontracting rights, approval requirements, pass-through obligations, and liability flow-downs.
* Map reliance on licensors, cloud providers, regulators or consents and whether failure by a third party is properly addressed.

### ****Step 14: Assignment, Novation and Change of Control****

* Assess transfer restrictions, consent mechanics, deemed consent timelines, and consequences of change of control.
* Flag asymmetries allowing counterparty freedom to assign while restricting the user.

### ****Step 15: Termination and Interdependency****

* Stress-test termination for convenience, for cause, insolvency and prolonged force majeure.
* For linked contracts, analyse cross-default, cross-termination, and cascading failure risk. Confirm survival of critical clauses and orderly exit obligations.

### ****Step 16: Force Majeure and Hardship****

* Review definitions covering pandemics, strikes, load shedding, regulatory action and supply chain shocks.
* Distinguish suspension vs excuse of performance, notice requirements, mitigation duties, and renegotiation triggers for hardship.

### ****Step 17: Dispute Resolution and Enforcement****

* Evaluate governing law, forum selection, arbitration vs litigation, interim relief availability, cost shifting, escalation steps.
* If cross-border, consider enforceability of judgments or awards and seat vs venue coherence.

### ****Step 18: Audit, Monitoring, Publicity and Reputation****

* Examine audit rights for scope, frequency, confidentiality, cost allocation and data access.
* Review publicity, branding, non-disparagement and gag clauses for reputational risk and whistleblowing carve-outs.

### ****Step 19: Entire Agreement and Integration****

* Check entire agreement, non-reliance, and order of precedence provisions.
* Protect critical pre-contract assurances by incorporating representations or collateral warranties where needed.

### ****Step 20: Future-Proofing and Indexation****

* Assess resilience to inflation, currency volatility, law or tech changes, and obsolescence.
* Recommend price indexation, review cycles, benchmarking, and tech refresh clauses where appropriate.

### ****Step 21: Highlight Clauses of Concern****

* Identify and list any clauses in the contract(s) that may be problematic, unclear, or unfavourable to the user, referencing clause numbers.
* Briefly explain why each clause may be of concern and tie back to Steps 5 to 20 where relevant.

### ****Step 22: Risk Register and Prioritisation****

* Produce a risk register with severity, likelihood, owner, mitigation path, and timeline.
* Include a simple heatmap and a top 10 issues list for executive consumption.

### ****Step 23: Negotiation Levers and Redline-Ready Proposals****

* Provide concrete bargaining levers, fallback positions, and sample redline language referencing the relevant clauses.
* Indicate which levers are high value, low cost vs high cost.

### ****Step 24: Suggest Omissions and Propose Clauses****

* List important clauses or protections that appear to be missing.
* For each omission, propose draft wording for a clause that could address the gap, tailored to the jurisdiction and commercial context.

## ****Output Format****

* Executive summary: top 10 issues, key contradictions, and must-fix items.
* Detailed findings: structured per Steps 4 to 20 with clause references.
* Risk register table and heatmap.
* Proposed clause drafts and redline-ready language.
* Action checklist with priorities, owners and deadlines.

The last step should have produced a clear list of proposed additional clauses with explanations. If it did not, say so explicitly so I can clarify or restart the process.
