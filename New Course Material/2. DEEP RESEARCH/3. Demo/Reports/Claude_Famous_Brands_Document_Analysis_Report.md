## Prepared for: Alterra Capital Deal Team

**Date:** 15 March 2026

**Historical coverage:** FY 2022 to FY 2025 (four years extractable from the document pack; FY 2020 and FY 2021 are not available from the attached reports)

## Limitations

The three annual reports provided contain the current year and one prior-year comparative. This yields audited data for FY 2022, FY 2023, FY 2024, and FY 2025. The questionnaire contemplates a six-year base (FY 2020–FY 2025), but FY 2020 and FY 2021 actuals are not available from these documents. FY 2020 (the COVID-impacted year ending February 2021) and FY 2021 should be sourced from older annual reports or the Integrated Annual Report five-year review if available. ## Q3: Revenue growth rate

**Base case: 3.2%**

| Year | FY 2022 | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (R000) | 6,476,354 | 7,443,924 | 8,023,793 | 8,282,653 |
| YoY growth | — | 14.9% | 7.8% | 3.2% |

- **4-year average growth (FY 2023–FY 2025):** 8.6%
- **Median growth:** 7.8%
- **Trend:** Decelerating sharply — from 14.9% (post-COVID recovery in FY 2023) to 3.2% in FY 2025, which is broadly in line with South African CPI.
- **Segment context (FY 2025):** SA contributed R7.63bn (92.1% of Group revenue), growing 3.1%. SADC grew 10.0% to R451m. UK declined 18.5% to R132m. AME grew 27.7% to R71m, albeit from a small base.
- **Document commentary:** The directors' report notes an expectation of "low growth and low consumer discretionary spend" in South Africa, offset by tailwinds including lower inflation, further interest rate cuts, and improved business confidence. Management is investing in delivery channels, smaller formats, and drive-throughs. The restaurant pipeline for Leading Brands is described as "healthy".
- **Recommended forward-looking value:** **4.0%–5.0%** for the base case. The 3.2% FY 2025 rate reflects a trough in consumer spending. With SA interest rate cuts flowing through, modest volume recovery is plausible, but systemic economic constraints (described extensively in the going concern note) limit upside. Supply chain revenue (70% of Group) is largely pass-through pricing to franchise partners, so revenue growth tracks food inflation plus modest volume. A 4.5% central assumption is defensible.
- **External data needed?** Yes — SA CPI food forecast for FY 2026–FY 2030 from SARB or BER; QSR sector growth benchmarks from Euromonitor or similar.

## Q4: Cost of sales (% of revenue)

**Base case: 57.2%**

| Year | FY 2022 | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost of sales (R000) | 3,564,258 | 4,267,922 | 4,639,859 | 4,737,309 |
| % of revenue | 55.0% | 57.3% | 57.8% | 57.2% |

- **4-year average:** 56.8%
- **Trend:** Rose from 55.0% in FY 2022 to 57.8% in FY 2024, then eased slightly to 57.2% in FY 2025. This is consistent with post-COVID input cost inflation that management has only partially passed on to consumers through menu price increases.
- **Document commentary:** The directors' report states the Group is "refurbishing our manufacturing plants with investments phased over the next three years" to "enhance capacity, processes and yields, while also reducing waste" and will "benchmark our processes, costs and margins to ensure efficiencies and reduce costs". The manufacturing segment margin improved from 9.0% to 11.0% in FY 2025, suggesting some early traction.
- **FY 2025 vs average:** The 57.2% base case is above the 4-year average of 56.8%, suggesting it is a conservative assumption but not an outlier.
- **Recommended forward-looking value:** **57.0%** for the base case. The manufacturing plant refurbishment programme should deliver modest yield improvements, but commodity cost pressures (particularly in a weaker-Rand environment) limit downside. Holding at approximately the FY 2025 level is reasonable; a range of 56.5%–57.5% captures realistic outcomes.
- **External data needed?** Yes — SA food commodity price outlook (beef, poultry, wheat, coffee); Rand exchange rate forecasts (relevant for imported inputs).

Stripping out the non-recurring GBK dividend and the put option remeasurement, underlying other income is approximately R35m, or 0.4% of revenue. ## Q8: Marketing expenses (% of revenue)

**Base case: 8.7%**

| Year | FY 2022 | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing expenses (R000) | 583,266 | 614,198 | 659,538 | 717,952 |
| % of revenue | 9.0% | 8.3% | 8.2% | 8.7% |

- **4-year average:** 8.5%
- **Trend:** Broadly stable with a slight uptick in FY 2025. Marketing expenses jumped by R58m (8.9% YoY) in FY 2025 against revenue growth of only 3.2%.
- **Document commentary:** Marketing fees revenue (franchise partner contributions) totalled R690.7m in FY 2025. Under the CPA-governed franchise model, marketing fund contributions are ring-fenced: they must be spent on marketing and any surplus is held as restricted cash (R44.1m at year-end). Thus, most of the marketing expense is effectively funded by franchisees and passes through the P&L with a near-neutral profit impact. The Group's own incremental marketing spend above franchise contributions is the true economic cost.
- **Key structural point:** Marketing revenue (R690.7m) was R27.2m less than marketing expense (R717.9m) in FY 2025, implying the Group absorbed approximately R27m of its own marketing spend. In FY 2024, the gap was wider (R677.5m revenue vs R659.5m expense, a R18m surplus held as restricted cash).
- **Recommended forward-looking value:** **8.5%–8.7%**. The marketing fund mechanism means this ratio largely tracks marketing fee revenue as a percentage of total revenue. The 8.7% FY 2025 level is reasonable; a slight reduction towards the 4-year average of 8.5% is defensible if management tightens the gap between marketing fund inflows and outlays.
- **External data needed?** No — the franchise agreement structure governs this line.

## Q9: Operations expenses (% of revenue)

**Base case: 21.4%**

| Year | FY 2022 | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operations expenses (R000) | 1,514,638 | 1,618,516 | 1,747,306 | 1,772,860 |
| % of revenue | 23.4% | 21.7% | 21.8% | 21.4% |

- **4-year average:** 22.1%
- **Trend:** Declining from 23.4% to 21.4%. This is the largest single improvement area in the Group's cost base over the period, driven by supply chain efficiency gains.
- **Document commentary:** The supply chain segment operating profit margin improved from 7.1% (FY 2024) to 7.7% (FY 2025), with manufacturing margins expanding from 9.0% to 11.0%. Logistics margins compressed from 1.9% to 1.4%, likely reflecting fuel and labour cost pressures. Employee costs in the supply chain segments (manufacturing plus logistics) totalled R522m in FY 2025. Electricity costs of R86m and depreciation of R70m are also material components.
- **Recommended forward-looking value:** **21.5%** for the base case. The downward trend is encouraging, but FY 2025 is already the lowest ratio in the series. The manufacturing plant refurbishment programme should deliver further yield improvements over FY 2026–FY 2028, but these are unlikely to more than offset electricity, wage, and fuel inflation. Holding the ratio approximately flat at the FY 2025 level is prudent.
- **External data needed?** Yes — SA electricity tariff outlook (Eskom/NERSA); SA minimum wage trajectory; diesel price forecasts.

## Q10: Impairment of intangible assets

**Base case: R(2,063)k**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Impairment (R000) | (25,090) | (40,643) | (12,889) | (2,063) |

- **Trend:** Declining sharply from the FY 2023 peak. FY 2022 included a major UK-related impairment; FY 2023 was elevated by further brand write-downs. FY 2024 and FY 2025 charges relate primarily to Signature brands (Fego Caffé conversion to the Leading Brands portfolio).
- **Document commentary:** Goodwill of R449m and trademarks/brand names of R346m remain on the balance sheet at FY 2025 (92% of intangible assets). They should instead be stress-tested in a downside scenario. If a downside overlay is needed, R10m–R15m per annum captures a normalised level.

## Q12: Finance costs

**Base case: R(152,078)k**

| Year | FY 2022 | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finance costs (R000) | (124,836) | (122,498) | (160,931) | (152,078) |

**Breakdown (FY 2025):** Interest on borrowings R117.1m; interest on lease liabilities R29.5m; other finance costs R5.5m.

- **Trend:** Rose sharply in FY 2024 (reflecting both increased borrowings and higher SA interest rates) and eased slightly in FY 2025 as the Group reduced net debt.
- **Debt profile (FY 2025):** Total borrowings of R1,140m, predominantly variable-rate (JIBAR + margins of 1.50%–2.15%). Weighted all-in cost of approximately 9.5%–10.0%. Lease liabilities of R298m. Interest rate swaps cover R420m notional at fixed rates of 7.57%–7.77%. Net debt/EBITDA improved from 1.13x to 0.89x. Financial covenants (leverage ≤2.25x, interest cover ≥3.00x) were satisfied.
- **Take-private implication:** This line will be **entirely replaced** by acquisition financing in a take-private. The current capital structure is irrelevant to the projection. The deal team must model finance costs based on the target leverage, LBO debt pricing, and amortisation schedule.
- **Recommended forward-looking value:** **Cannot be set from the document pack.** This is a function of the acquisition capital structure. For a placeholder, a PE-typical leverage of 3.0x–4.0x EBITDA at current SA lending rates (JIBAR + 3.0%–4.0%) would imply finance costs materially higher than the current R152m.
- **External data needed?** Yes — acquisition financing term sheet; SA JIBAR forward curve; sponsor target leverage.

| NCI % | 10.6% | 5.6% | 5.4% | 6.3% |

- **4-year average:** 7.0%
- **Trend:** Volatile, driven by the profitability mix of NCI-bearing subsidiaries. The FY 2022 outlier (10.6%) reflects a year when Group profit was depressed but NCI subsidiaries (particularly Famous Brands Cheese Company) remained profitable.

## Q21–Q23: Analyst input variables

### Q21: Terminal growth rate

- **Cannot be derived from the document pack.**
- **Relevant historical context:** Revenue CAGR over FY 2022–FY 2025 was 8.6%, but this includes the post-COVID recovery. The most recent year's growth (3.2%) is more representative of the current run-rate. SA long-term GDP growth expectations are typically 1.5%–2.5%, and long-term CPI is targeted at 3%–6% (midpoint 4.5%).
- **Management's impairment assumptions:** Terminal growth rates used in value-in-use calculations are 1.6% (domestic) and 2.5% (UK).
- **Suggested range:** 3.0%–4.5%, aligned with SA long-term nominal GDP growth. Management's own 1.6% terminal growth rate is conservative and suitable for impairment testing but may understate the long-term franchise system growth for a take-private valuation.
- **Recommended source:** SARB Monetary Policy Review; SA National Treasury medium-term fiscal outlook; IMF World Economic Outlook for SA.

### Q22: WACC / Discount rate

- **Cannot be derived from the document pack.**
- **Relevant historical context:** Management uses post-tax discount rates of 16.7%–18.2% for domestic intangibles and 13.1% for UK intangibles in their impairment testing (Note 2). Pre-tax equivalents are 17.4%–18.9% (domestic) and 13.3% (UK). These rates include an equity risk premium, country risk premium, and company-specific risk adjustments.
- **Suggested range:** 14%–18% for a PE discount rate / cost of equity. WACC will be lower depending on the debt/equity mix and cost of debt in the acquisition structure.
- **Recommended source:** Damodaran's SA equity risk premium data; SA government bond yields (risk-free rate proxy); comparable transaction implied returns.

### Q23: Exit EV/EBITDA multiple

- **Cannot be derived from the document pack.**
- **Relevant historical context:** FY 2025 EBITDA was R1,120m (operating profit of R914m + depreciation R188m + amortisation R17m + impairment R2m). EBITDA margin was 13.5% of revenue (FY 2024: 12.9%). The EBITDA margin has been expanding, which supports a constructive exit narrative.
- **Historical EBITDA margin range:** 12.0%–13.5% over FY 2022–FY 2025.
- **Suggested range:** 7.0x–10.0x. JSE-listed QSR/franchise comparables and recent SA take-private transactions should be benchmarked. The franchise model (asset-light, high cash conversion) typically commands a premium to corporate restaurant operators.
- **Recommended source:** JSE peer trading multiples (Spur Corporation, Taste Holdings residual); recent SA PE deal multiples from DealLogic or Mergermarket; international franchise comps (Restaurant Brands International, Yum! Brands — adjusted for SA risk).
