## Sector structure and recent conditions

**Facts (market size proxy and growth)**: The formal foodservice market is large and has returned to growth in real terms in 2025. In the official P6420 survey, **total real income** for the food and beverages industry was **R82,439.2 million** in 2024 (constant 2019 prices). citeturn25view1 In **October 2025**, the industry’s real income was **8.4 per cent higher** than October 2024, with restaurants and coffee shops up **10.1 per cent** and takeaway/fast‑food outlets up **9.6 per cent** year‑on‑year that month. citeturn25view0

**Facts (segment mix)**: The same official dataset indicates that, in the three months ended October 2025, restaurants and coffee shops represented **47.4 per cent** of measured income, takeaway and fast‑food outlets **39.0 per cent**, and catering services **13.6 per cent**. citeturn25view0

**Inferences (what this means commercially)**: The measured mix and the faster growth rate of takeaway/fast‑food outlets support an evidence‑based view that **value and convenience formats** are structurally advantaged in the current cycle (even if casual dining can still grow via brand reinvention and loyalty). citeturn25view0turn19view0 This should inform underwriting assumptions: defendable growth is more likely to come from QSR throughput, delivery, and efficient formats than from premium sit‑down dining expansion. citeturn12search0turn25view0

**Facts (franchising as a structural feature)**: Franchising remains a meaningful operating model in South Africa. The entity["organization","Franchise Association of South Africa","franchising trade body"] cites its 2023 franchise survey (sponsored by Absa) as finding fast foods and QSR restaurants to be the largest franchise sector at **19 per cent** (by sector share within franchising). (FASA web article, 18 June 2024.) citeturn22search6

**Facts (macro and consumer pressure)**: Inflation and rates have eased versus prior peaks, but cost volatility remains. entity["organization","Statistics South Africa","national statistics office"] reports **average inflation of 3.2 per cent in 2025** (the lowest in 21 years), while noting sharp intra‑basket movements such as meat inflation. citeturn23view0 The entity["organization","South African Reserve Bank","central bank"] reports a policy rate environment of **repo 6.75 per cent** (January 2026 MPC statement) and a prime lending rate of **10.25 per cent** (current market rates page). citeturn13search1turn13search2

**Facts (energy reliability as an operating variable)**: Load shedding has improved from prior highs, but it remains a salient risk for hospitality. The entity["organization","Council for Scientific and Industrial Research","south african research council"] reported **281 days without load shedding in 2024**, and highlighted a sharp reduction in load shedding in early 2025 versus early 2024. citeturn4search1turn4search9 Famous Brands’ FY2024 reporting explicitly cites “elevated levels of severe load shedding” increasing operating costs. (Famous Brands Integrated Annual Report 2024 Operational Review.) citeturn10view1

**Inferences (PE underwriting)**: A credible base case should assume (i) **consumer pressure persists** even with lower inflation (because high unemployment, high debt servicing, and food cost bursts constrain discretionary spend), and (ii) the sector remains investable for scaled players with strong value propositions and franchise discipline. citeturn19view0turn23view0turn22search6
