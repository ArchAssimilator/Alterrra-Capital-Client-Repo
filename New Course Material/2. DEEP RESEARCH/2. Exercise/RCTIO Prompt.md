# RCTIO PROMPT FOR COMPETITIVE DEEP RESEARCH

## Role

You are a senior private equity research analyst preparing an investment-committee-ready competitive landscape briefing on Famous Brands Limited (JSE: FBR). Be precise, sceptical, evidence-led, and commercially focused.

## Context

Alterra Capital is assessing a potential take-private of Famous Brands. The internal financial model is complete. What is needed now is an external view of the South African restaurant and food-service competitive landscape, centred on Famous Brands.

The report will be used directly in IC materials. Accuracy, source quality, triangulation, and relevance matter more than breadth. Prioritise public sources from 2022 onward. Use older sources only if still necessary and label them clearly.

Prioritise sources in this order:
1. Company filings, annual reports, investor presentations, and regulatory disclosures
2. Government and regulator sources
3. Reputable industry data and trade bodies
4. Established financial and business press
5. Specialist trade press and other contextual sources

Do not rely on a single source for investment-critical claims where independent confirmation is available.

## Task

Produce a structured competitive landscape report on the South African QSR and casual dining sector, framed around Famous Brands' position and what it means for a potential PE acquisition.

Address the following five research areas:

1. **Sector overview**
Establish market size, growth, outlet footprint, and major structural trends in the categories most relevant to Famous Brands.

2. **Competitive map**
Identify the most relevant competitors by segment and explain how they compete with Famous Brands on scale, format, price point, customer proposition, distribution model, and delivery capability.

Include at minimum:
- QSR and mass-market franchised peers: KFC, McDonald's, Burger King South Africa, Nando's, Chicken Licken, Spur/RocoMamas
- Casual dining, coffee, and adjacent formats: Mugg & Bean peers such as Tashas, Vida e Caffe, Doppio Zero, and other material concepts where relevant
- Delivery-first, platform-led, dark kitchen, or convenience-led operators where they represent a credible structural threat

3. **Market share and competitive dynamics**
Assess where Famous Brands leads, defends, weakens, or faces disruption. Focus on brand momentum, outlet counts, franchise economics, pricing power, digital maturity, and channel mix.

4. **Structural headwinds and tailwinds**
Analyse the main external forces affecting the sector, including consumer affordability, food inflation, wage pressure, electricity reliability, delivery economics, health and dietary shifts, and relevant regulation.

5. **PE and strategic investor activity**
Summarise relevant M&A, take-private, minority investment, or platform-building activity in South African or comparable food-service sectors since 2020, and explain what it signals for valuation, appetite, or strategic logic.

## Instructions

1. Use only verifiable public information.
2. Cite sources inline using source name and date.
3. Distinguish clearly between `Facts`, `Inferences`, and `Not verified`.
4. If exact data is unavailable, say so. Do not invent or loosely estimate unsupported figures.
5. Cross-check competitor claims against independent reporting where possible.
6. Do not spend space retelling Famous Brands' own equity story unless needed for comparison.
7. Apply a PE lens throughout. For each major finding, state why it matters for thesis, risk, value creation, exit, or valuation.
8. Focus on South Africa first. Use international analogues only where they clarify local dynamics, and label them as contextual.
9. If evidence conflicts, show the conflict and explain which view is more credible.
10. Keep the analysis concise and decision-oriented. Avoid generic commentary that does not affect the investment view.

## Output

Return the report in the following order:

### 1. Executive Summary

Keep to approximately one page. Cover:
- overall view on sector attractiveness
- Famous Brands' relative competitive position
- 3-5 most important investment implications

### 2. Sector Structure

Summarise:
- market size and growth
- relevant segment structure
- key recent changes in market conditions

### 3. Competitive Map

Provide:
- a segmented list of the most relevant competitors
- a short explanation of why each matters

### 4. Competitor Profiles

Write one concise paragraph for each of 8-12 material competitors. For each competitor, cover:
- brand/format positioning
- approximate scale or footprint if available
- areas of strength
- areas of weakness
- relevance to Famous Brands

### 5. Competitive Positioning Table

Include a markdown table comparing Famous Brands with key peers across:
- scale
- brand strength
- menu breadth / category coverage
- vertical integration / supply chain strength
- digital and delivery maturity
- franchise model quality / franchisee economics
- affordability / value positioning
- apparent resilience under current consumer conditions

Use directional ratings where hard data is unavailable and label them clearly.

### 6. Structural Headwinds and Tailwinds

Separate into:
- headwinds
- tailwinds

For each item, add one line on why it matters to a PE owner.

### 7. PE / Strategic Activity

Summarise the most relevant transactions or investment activity since 2020 and state what each implies for:
- sector attractiveness
- valuation context
- potential exit routes

### 8. Key Risks and Opportunities for a PE Buyer

Provide a short list of the most material:
- risks
- opportunities

### 9. Evidence Table

Include a final markdown table with these columns:

`Claim | Evidence summary | Source | Source type | Support type | Confidence`

Use:
- `Support type`: `Direct confirmation`, `Triangulation`, or `Contextual support`
- `Confidence`: `High`, `Medium`, or `Low`

### 10. Open Questions and Manual Follow-ups

List the few remaining items the deal team should verify manually before relying on the report in IC.
