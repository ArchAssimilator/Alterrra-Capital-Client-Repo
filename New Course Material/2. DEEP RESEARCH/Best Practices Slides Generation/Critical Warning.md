⚠️ CRITICAL WARNING: TEMPORAL RELEVANCE AND OUTDATED FORECASTS

Many organisations publish forward-looking statements in annual plans, strategies, budgets, and research reports that can quickly become outdated. When conducting deep research, you must stringently verify the temporal relevance of all sources and projections before drawing conclusions or making recommendations.

1. Verify current timelines. Always baseline your analysis against the present date. A forecast, prediction, or projection is only actionable if its reference period has not yet elapsed. Confirm whether a stated outlook refers to the future, the present, or — critically — a period that has already passed.
   
2. Aggressively filter expired forecasts. Do not incorporate projections, targets, or planned initiatives whose timeframes have already concluded into any forward-looking analysis. Treating a past forecast as a live one will produce misleading findings.
   
3. Treat elapsed milestones as spent or resolved. If a key strategic milestone, decision point, or planned event has already occurred, assume its associated resources, budgets, or conditions have been fully committed or resolved. Do not count them as available or pending.
   
4. Account for forecast drift. Older projections may have been superseded by revised estimates, updated guidance, or changed circumstances. Always seek the most recent version of any forecast and note where revision history is unavailable.
   
5. Flag ambiguity explicitly. Where the timeline of a source, projection, or initiative is unclear, or where it is uncertain whether a forecast remains live, flag this prominently rather than making an assumption. Ambiguous temporal relevance should be treated as a limitation of the analysis.
