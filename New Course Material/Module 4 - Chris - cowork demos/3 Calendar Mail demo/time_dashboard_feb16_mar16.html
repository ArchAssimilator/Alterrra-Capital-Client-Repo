Process Improvement
Load Management Recommendations
1
Cap consecutive delivery days at 3

Insert a "buffer day" every 3 delivery days for admin, prep, and recovery. Use this day for follow-up emails, content refinement, and strategic thinking. This alone will meaningfully reduce burnout risk across a multi-week programme.

2
Block one BD/strategy half-day per week

Reserve Tuesday or Thursday mornings (08:00–12:00) for business development — prospect outreach, proposal writing, and partner meetings. Protecting this time in the calendar before delivery blocks fill it is critical for pipeline continuity.

3
Front-load prep before travel trips

For the Cape Town run, prep should be completed no later than the Thursday before departure (Mar 5), not on the weekend during the trip itself (Mar 11 was a Saturday). Consider adding a "trip prep" event 7 days before any out-of-town delivery.

4
Protect Kita week mornings and afternoons

During Kita weeks, flag 07:00–08:30 and 17:00–19:00 as unavailable in your calendar. This reduces scheduling conflicts for school logistics and pick-ups, and signals the boundary clearly to anyone who books your calendar.

5
Explore venue proximity or accommodation closer to delivery sites

27h of travel in 29 days (including 2h/day commutes to JHB venues) is a significant drain. Staying close to the venue for multi-day JHB engagements — or negotiating venue change to a more central location — could reclaim 10–15 hours per month.

6
Increase industry engagement to 2–3h/week

Only 1.5h of industry/networking time in 29 days is insufficient for a thought-leadership business. Prioritise at least one webinar, podcast appearance, or strategic conversation weekly — even during heavy delivery months — to maintain market visibility.
