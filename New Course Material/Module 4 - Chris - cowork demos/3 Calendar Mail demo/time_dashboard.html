Load Management Recommendations
1

Protect 1 "No-Delivery" Day Per Week

Designate a fixed day each week (e.g. Thursday) as a strategy, BD, and admin day. Block it in your calendar as recurring and treat it like a client commitment. Even a single protected day per week would add ~8–9h of strategic capacity — nearly tripling your current 3h/month meeting total.

2

Build a Recovery Day Into Every Travel Engagement

The Cape Town trip ended Saturday 14 March with a 07:20 flight, and the next delivery began Monday 16 March. Future multi-day trips should include a mandatory recovery buffer — at minimum one day for admin catch-up, debrief, and re-acclimation. This pays dividends in delivery quality and personal sustainability.

3

Batch and Delegate Prep Work

24h of prep time across 3 days (including a Sunday) suggests prep is happening reactively. Consider templating recurring masterclass components so prep is reduced to a 2–3h refresh, and explore whether a team member or EA could handle logistics, materials printing, and venue coordination.

4

Schedule Monthly BD Meetings Proactively

With only 3 external meetings in 4 weeks, relationship development is at risk. Set a minimum quota of 4–6 external touchpoints per month (calls, coffees, check-ins). Pre-schedule these in your calendar at the start of each month before delivery slots fill in around them.

5

Cap Consecutive Delivery Runs at 3 Days Max

Weeks 2 and 4 both feature 4 consecutive full-day delivery days. Research on cognitive performance shows significant quality degradation after day 3 of intensive facilitation. Consider restructuring multi-day cohort programmes to include a mid-programme break or splitting across two weeks where client needs allow.
