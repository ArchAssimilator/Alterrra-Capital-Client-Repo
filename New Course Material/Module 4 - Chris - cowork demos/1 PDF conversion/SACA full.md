# Companies Act 71 of 2008 (South Africa)

*Government Gazette, Republic of South Africa — Vol. 526, Cape Town, 9 April 2009, No. 32121*

*It is hereby notified that the President has assented to the following Act, which is hereby published for general information: No. 71 of 2008: Companies Act, 2008.*

---

## Overview

The **Companies Act 71 of 2008** is the cornerstone of South African corporate law. It governs the entire lifecycle of a company — from incorporation and governance through to dissolution or business rescue. The Act is notable for:

- Introducing a flexible, enabling framework for company incorporation (replacing the prescriptive 1973 Act)
- Establishing the **Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC)** as the primary regulator
- Introducing formal **business rescue** proceedings as an alternative to liquidation
- Codifying directors' duties and expanding liability for reckless conduct
- Providing enhanced protection for minority shareholders and whistleblowers
- Creating a modern **Takeover Regulation Panel** for affected transactions

## Chapter 1 — Interpretation, Purpose and Application (ss. 1–10)

### Key Provisions

**Section 1 — Definitions:** Defines key terms used throughout the Act, including "company," "director," "securities," "Memorandum of Incorporation," "prescribed officer," "beneficial interest," and many others.

**Section 7 — Purposes of the Act:** The Act's purposes include:
- Promoting compliance with the Bill of Rights in applying company law
- Promoting the development of the South African economy
- Encouraging entrepreneurship and enterprise efficiency
- Creating flexibility and simplicity in the formation and maintenance of companies
- Providing a predictable and effective environment for the efficient regulation of companies
- Promoting the interests of shareholders and protecting third parties
- Establishing a better balance of rights and obligations of shareholders and company management

**Section 8 — Classes of companies:** Distinguishes between:
- **Profit companies:** Private companies (Pty Ltd), Personal liability companies, Public companies (Ltd), State owned companies (SOC)
- **Non-profit companies (NPC)**

**Section 9 — Modified application with respect to state-owned companies**

**Section 10 — Modified application with respect to non-profit companies**

## Chapter 2 — Formation, Administration and Dissolution of Companies (ss. 11–83)

### Part A — Reservation and Registration of Company Names (ss. 11–12)

**Section 11 — Criteria for names of companies:** A company name must not be the same as or confusingly similar to another company name, trademark, or be offensive or contrary to public policy.

**Section 12 — Reservation of name for later use:** A person may apply to reserve a company name before incorporating.

### Part B — Incorporation and Legal Status (ss. 13–22)

**Section 13 — Right to incorporate company:** Any person has the right to incorporate a profit or non-profit company.

**Section 14 — Registration of company:** A company is incorporated by filing a Notice of Incorporation (NoI) and a Memorandum of Incorporation (MoI) with the Commission.

**Section 15 — Memorandum of Incorporation (MoI), shareholder agreements and rules of company:** The MoI is the foundational governance document of a company. Shareholder agreements may supplement but not contradict the MoI.

**Section 19 — Legal status of companies:** A company is a juristic person with perpetual succession, distinct from its shareholders.

**Section 20 — Validity of company actions:** A company's actions are not void merely because they are beyond the company's stated purposes (ultra vires).

**Section 22 — Reckless trading prohibited:** A company must not carry on its business recklessly, with gross negligence, with intent to defraud, or for any fraudulent purpose.

### Part C — Transparency, Accountability and Integrity (ss. 23–34)

**Section 26 — Access to company records:** Members of the public are entitled to inspect certain company records including the MoI, shareholder register, and annual financial statements.

**Section 29 — Financial statements:** Financial statements must not be false or misleading; must satisfy prescribed standards.

**Section 30 — Annual financial statements:** Companies must prepare annual financial statements within 6 months after the end of the financial year.

**Section 33 — Annual return:** Every company must file an annual return with the Commission.

### Part D — Capitalisation of Profit Companies (ss. 35–48)

**Section 36 — Authorisation for shares:** A company's MoI must set out the authorised shares.

**Section 41 — Shareholder approval for issuing shares in certain cases:** Board approval and/or shareholder approval may be required for certain share issues.

**Section 44 — Financial assistance for subscription of securities:** The board may authorise financial assistance for the subscription of securities under certain conditions.

**Section 45 — Loans or other financial assistance to directors:** Restricted; requires special board resolution and shareholder notification.

**Section 46 — Distributions must be authorised by board:** A company may not make a distribution unless the board has passed a solvency and liquidity resolution.

### Part E — Securities Registration and Transfer (ss. 49–56)

Regulates the issuing of share certificates, the securities register, and the transfer of certificated and uncertificated securities.

### Part F — Governance of Companies (ss. 57–78)

**Section 58 — Shareholder right to be represented by proxy:** A shareholder may appoint any person as proxy.

**Section 61 — Shareholders meetings:** Annual general meetings (AGMs) are required for public companies. Requirements for notice, quorum, and conduct of meetings are prescribed.

**Section 65 — Shareholder resolutions:** Distinguishes between ordinary resolutions (majority >50%), special resolutions (75% or more), and resolutions requiring specific percentages.

**Section 66 — Board, directors and prescribed officers:** The business and affairs of a company must be managed by, or under the direction of, the board.

**Section 69 — Ineligibility and disqualification of persons to be director or prescribed officer:** A director may be ineligible or disqualified by reason of various grounds including being an unrehabilitated insolvent, having been convicted of fraud or dishonesty, being of unsound mind, or being declared delinquent by a court.

**Section 71 — Removal of directors:** The board may remove a director by ordinary resolution; the company may also remove a director by ordinary resolution of shareholders.

**Section 75 — Director's personal financial interests:** A director who has a personal financial interest in a matter must disclose this and recuse themselves from the board discussion and vote.

**Section 76 — Standards of directors' conduct:** A director must:
- Act in good faith and in the best interests of the company
- Act with the degree of care, skill, and diligence that may reasonably be expected of a person carrying out the same functions
- Not use the position of director or confidential information for personal gain

**Section 77 — Liability of directors and prescribed officers:** A director is liable for loss suffered by the company where the director acted with negligence, recklessness, or with intent to defraud.

**Section 78 — Indemnification and directors' insurance:** A company may indemnify directors in certain circumstances; directors may be covered by directors' and officers' (D&O) insurance.

### Part G — Winding-up of Solvent Companies and Deregistering (ss. 79–83)

Regulates voluntary and court-ordered winding-up of solvent companies, dissolution, and removal from the companies register.

## Chapter 3 — Enhanced Accountability and Transparency (ss. 84–94)

### Part B — Company Secretary (ss. 86–89)

**Section 86 — Mandatory appointment of company secretary:** Public companies and state-owned companies must appoint a company secretary.

**Section 88 — Duties of company secretary:** Includes ensuring that the company complies with its MoI, the Act, keeping minutes, filing required documents, and providing guidance to directors on their duties.

### Part C — Auditors (ss. 90–93)

**Section 90 — Appointment of auditor:** Public companies, state-owned companies, and certain other companies must appoint a registered auditor.

**Section 92 — Rotation of auditors:** Prescribed rules on rotation of audit firms and individual auditors.

### Part D — Audit Committees (s. 94)

**Section 94 — Audit committees:** Public companies and state-owned companies must have an audit committee comprising at least three independent non-executive directors. The audit committee nominates the external auditor, determines remuneration of the auditor, and prepares a report for the annual financial statements.

## Chapter 4 — Public Offerings of Company Securities (ss. 95–111)

Governs the offer of company securities (shares, debentures, etc.) to the public. Key provisions include:

**Section 96 — Offers that are not offers to public:** Certain limited distributions (to 50 or fewer people or to qualifying investors) are exempt.

**Section 99 — General restrictions on offers to public:** Requires a registered prospectus filed with the Commission before a public offer.

**Section 100 — Requirements concerning prospectus:** Sets out mandatory content of a prospectus.

**Section 104 — Liability for untrue statements in prospectus:** Civil liability attaches to directors and persons named in the prospectus for untrue statements.

## Chapter 5 — Fundamental Transactions, Takeovers and Offers (ss. 112–127)

### Part A — Approval for Certain Fundamental Transactions (ss. 112–116)

**Section 112 — Proposals to dispose of all or greater part of assets or undertaking:** Requires shareholder approval (special resolution) to dispose of all or a greater part of a company's assets.

**Section 113 — Proposals for amalgamation or merger**

**Section 114 — Proposals for scheme of arrangement**

**Section 115 — Required approval for transactions:** Requires special resolution approval (75%) and court approval in certain cases; dissenting shareholders have appraisal rights.

### Part B — Authority of Panel and Takeover Regulations (ss. 117–120)

Establishes the Takeover Regulation Panel's authority over "affected transactions" (transactions involving listed companies or companies with more than 10 shareholders).

### Part C — Regulation of Affected Transactions and Offers (ss. 121–127)

**Section 123 — Mandatory offers:** A person acquiring 35% or more of a company's voting rights must make a mandatory offer to all other shareholders at the highest price paid in the preceding 12 months.

**Section 124 — Compulsory acquisitions and squeeze-out:** An acquirer holding ≥90% of a company's shares following an offer may compulsorily acquire remaining shares.

**Section 127 — Prohibited dealings before and during an offer:** Insider trading and market manipulation in the context of takeover offers are prohibited.

## Chapter 6 — Business Rescue and Compromise with Creditors (ss. 128–155)

This chapter introduces a formal **business rescue** regime for financially distressed companies — a significant innovation over the 1973 Act.

### Part A — Business Rescue Proceedings (ss. 128–137)

**Section 128 — Definitions:** "Business rescue" means proceedings to facilitate the rehabilitation of a company that is financially distressed. "Financially distressed" means it appears reasonably unlikely that the company will be able to pay all its debts as they become due and payable within the immediately ensuing six months, or it appears reasonably likely that the company will become insolvent within the immediately ensuing six months.

**Section 129 — Company resolution to begin business rescue proceedings:** The board of a company may resolve to begin business rescue proceedings if the company is financially distressed; a business rescue practitioner is then appointed.

**Section 131 — Court order to begin business rescue proceedings:** Any affected person (creditor, shareholder, employee or their representative, or the Commission) may apply to court for a business rescue order.

**Section 133 — General moratorium on legal proceedings against company:** During business rescue, no legal proceedings may be commenced or continued against the company without the written consent of the practitioner or leave of court.

**Section 135 — Post-commencement finance:** The company may obtain financing during business rescue; such financing has priority over pre-existing unsecured claims (but not secured claims).

### Part B — Practitioner's Functions and Terms of Appointment (ss. 138–143)

**Section 138 — Qualifications of practitioners:** A business rescue practitioner must be a licensed insolvency practitioner or meet other prescribed qualifications.

**Section 140 — General powers and duties of practitioner:** The practitioner has full management control of the company during business rescue, including the power to remove directors.

### Parts C–D — Rights of Affected Persons and Business Rescue Plan (ss. 144–154)

- Employees retain their employment and are entitled to participate in the process
- Creditors have the right to vote on the business rescue plan
- The practitioner must develop a business rescue plan within 25 business days of appointment
- The plan is adopted if approved by creditors holding the majority of voting interest

### Part E — Compromise with Creditors (s. 155)

**Section 155:** A company may reach a compromise arrangement with creditors without entering formal business rescue, subject to court approval.

## Chapter 7 — Remedies and Enforcement (ss. 156–184)

### Part A — General Principles (ss. 156–159)

**Section 157 — Extended standing to apply for remedies:** Any director, shareholder, employee, or other affected person may apply to court for remedies under the Act.

**Section 158 — Remedies to promote purpose of Act:** Courts must develop the law of remedies in line with the Act's purposes.

**Section 159 — Protection for whistleblowers:** Any person who in good faith discloses information relating to conduct that is an offence under the Act is protected from civil, criminal, or administrative liability.

### Part B — Rights to Seek Specific Remedies (ss. 160–165)

**Section 162 — Application to declare director delinquent or under probation:** A court may declare a director delinquent (permanent disqualification) or under probation (limited disqualification) for gross negligence, wilful misconduct, or breach of trust.

**Section 163 — Relief from oppressive or prejudicial conduct:** A shareholder or director may apply to court for relief if the affairs of the company are being conducted in a manner oppressive, unfairly prejudicial, or that unfairly disregards the interests of that shareholder or director.

**Section 164 — Dissenting shareholders' appraisal rights:** If a fundamental transaction is proposed and a shareholder votes against it, that shareholder may demand fair value for their shares (appraisal rights).

**Section 165 — Derivative actions:** A shareholder (or other affected person) may bring a legal action in the name of the company against its directors for breach of duty, subject to court leave.

### Part C — Voluntary Resolution of Disputes (ss. 166–167)

Encourages parties to resolve disputes through alternative dispute resolution (ADR) before approaching the courts or the Tribunal.

### Part D — Complaints to Commission or Panel (ss. 168–175)

Any person may file a complaint with the CIPC or Takeover Regulation Panel. The Commission may investigate and issue compliance notices; non-compliance with a compliance notice may result in administrative fines.

**Section 175 — Administrative fines:** Maximum fine of 10% of the company's annual turnover.

### Parts E–F — Powers to Support Investigations; Companies Tribunal Adjudication (ss. 176–184)

The Commission and inspectors have powers of summons, search and seizure. The Companies Tribunal adjudicates certain disputes with binding decisions.

---

## Chapter 8 — Regulatory Agencies and Administration of Act (ss. 185–212)

### Part A — Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) (ss. 185–192)

**Section 185 — Establishment of CIPC:** The Commission is established as a juristic person and is the primary regulator under the Act.

**Section 186 — Commission objectives:** Promote compliance with the Act; promote education and information relating to the Act; maintain a register of all companies; investigate complaints; enforce compliance.

**Section 187 — Functions of Commission:** Include registering companies and company names, receiving annual returns, maintaining the companies register, and investigating complaints.

### Part B — Companies Tribunal (ss. 193–195)

An independent Tribunal to adjudicate disputes, applications, and complaints arising under the Act, as an alternative to the courts.

### Part C — Takeover Regulation Panel (ss. 196–202)

An independent panel that regulates affected transactions (takeovers and mergers) involving regulated companies.

### Part D — Financial Reporting Standards Council (ss. 203–204)

Advises the Minister on financial reporting standards.

---

## Chapter 9 — Offences, Miscellaneous Matters and General Provisions (ss. 213–225)

**Section 213 — Breach of confidence:** Disclosure of confidential information relating to the affairs of a company is an offence.

**Section 214 — False statements, reckless conduct and non-compliance:** Making false statements, reckless conduct, and non-compliance with the Act are criminal offences.

**Section 216 — Penalties:** Fines and/or imprisonment for offences under the Act.

**Section 218 — Civil actions:** Conduct in breach of the Act is actionable by any person who has suffered loss or damage as a result.

**Section 223 — Regulations:** The Minister may make regulations for any matter that may or must be prescribed under the Act.

**Section 225 — Short title and commencement:** This Act is called the Companies Act 2008 and came into operation on a date to be fixed by the President by proclamation.

## Key Schedules

### Schedule 1 — Non-Profit Companies (NPCs)

Non-profit companies must have a specific stated object directed at public benefit or another non-profit purpose. NPCs may not distribute income or property to incorporators, members, directors, or officers. On dissolution, remaining property must be distributed to another NPC or similar organisation.

### Schedule 2 — Conversion of Close Corporations to Companies

Close corporations (the previous small business vehicle in South Africa) could convert to private companies under this schedule. New close corporations can no longer be formed under the Act (they were phased out).

### Schedule 3 — Amendment of Laws

Amends the Close Corporations Act 1984 and other legislation to align with the new Act.

### Schedule 5 — Transitional Arrangements

Provides for the continuation of pre-existing companies, pending filings, conversion of share capital, and general preservation of prior regulatory actions and orders.

## Key Concepts Summary

| Concept | Summary |
|---|---|
| Memorandum of Incorporation (MoI) | Founding document; can alter default Act provisions subject to limitations |
| Business Rescue | Formal rehabilitation proceedings for financially distressed companies; moratorium on creditor actions |
| Solvency and Liquidity Test | Required before any distribution; assets must exceed liabilities and company must be able to pay debts for 12 months |
| Business Judgement Rule | Directors protected from liability if they acted in good faith, on adequate information, with no conflict of interest |
| Appraisal Rights | Dissenting shareholders in fundamental transactions may demand fair value for their shares |
| Mandatory Offer | Acquiring ≥35% of votes in an affected company triggers obligation to offer for all shares |
| Delinquent Director | Court declaration permanently disqualifying a director from serving on a board |
| CIPC | Companies and Intellectual Property Commission — primary regulator and registrar |
| Derivative Action | Shareholder lawsuit brought in the name of the company against directors for breach of duty |
| Pre-emptive Rights | Shareholders have first right to subscribe for new share issues (default; alterable by MoI) |
