### Option 1: Opportunity Triage Analyst

**One-line pitch:**
A GPT/Gem configured to screen any new investment opportunity using a firm's proprietary triage framework — returning a structured pass/flag/progress recommendation in under 60 seconds.

**PE workflow it replicates:**
Deal origination and first-pass screening. Before a deal reaches an analyst's desk for real work, it must pass a rapid relevance filter. In practice this is done by a senior partner who has internalised the firm's thesis, sector focus, size criteria, and red-line exclusions. A custom GPT encodes that filter and applies it consistently at scale.

**Famous Brands angle:**
The triage prompt (`1b. Prompt_Opportunity_Triage.md`) was built directly for this case. Participants can see the before/after: feeding the Famous Brands 2025 Integrated Report into a plain chat session vs. feeding it into their triage GPT with the firm's criteria baked in. The structured output (Stage 0–5 framework, provisional recommendations, missing evidence flags) appears instantly and matches what a senior analyst spent hours producing.

**System prompt skeleton:**
```
You are a private equity investment analyst at [FIRM NAME], a South African PE firm
focused on [sector focus, e.g. consumer, industrials, financial services] investments
in southern Africa.

When given a company document (annual report, CIM, teaser, or news article), you
evaluate it against the firm's screening criteria across five stages:

STAGE 0: Business Snapshot — What is this company? What is the transaction context?
STAGE 1: Business Quality — Is this a good business? Evidence for/against.
STAGE 2: Sponsorability — Where can a PE firm add value? What is the value creation
          thesis?
STAGE 3: Financial Conversion — Does accounting profit turn into real cash?
STAGE 4: Risk Concentration — What are the deal-breaker risks?
STAGE 5: Screening Recommendation — Pass / Flag for clarification / Reject.
          With supporting and limiting factors listed.

FIRM CRITERIA (edit these to match your fund):
- Target equity ticket: R500m–R2bn
- Geography: South Africa and SADC primary; no exposure above 20% to single
  markets outside SA
- Sectors: Consumer, Logistics, Business Services — NO resources, biotech, or
  early-stage
- Model preference: Asset-light or franchise-led preferred; avoid pure capex-heavy
  manufacturing
- Red lines: Government dependency >30% revenue, litigation overhang, family
  conflict of interest in governance

OUTPUT FORMAT:
- Stage assessments with evidence and missing evidence bullets
- Provisional rating per stage (Attractive / Mixed / Weak / Insufficient data)
- Final recommendation with 3 supporting factors and 3 limiting factors
- Top 5 diligence questions before advancing the deal

Always note what evidence is missing and what you would need to increase conviction.
Do not speculate beyond what the documents show.
```

**Knowledge base:**
- Upload: `1b. Prompt_Opportunity_Triage.md` (the master triage framework)
- Optionally upload: `2a. 2025_extracted_details_Claude_CLI.md` (so participants can test asking "summarise what the triage found on Famous Brands")
- Participants write their own firm criteria in the system prompt — this is the learning moment

**Demo output:**
Participants paste a two-paragraph teaser for a hypothetical South African logistics company. The GPT returns a structured 5-stage assessment with evidence bullets, a provisional recommendation, and a diligence question list — in under 30 seconds. The room sees immediately that this is not a chatbot answer; it is a structured analytical output that follows the firm's framework every time.

**Build time estimate:** 12–15 minutes (5 min to read the system prompt skeleton, 5 min to customise firm criteria, 3 min to upload knowledge file and test with sample input)

### Option 2: IC Memo Drafter

**One-line pitch:**
A GPT/Gem that takes raw extracted financial data and triage notes and drafts a first-cut Investment Committee memorandum in the firm's house style — halving the analyst's memo-writing time.

**PE workflow it replicates:**
IC memo preparation is one of the most time-intensive tasks in PE deal execution. An analyst typically has 2–3 days to convert due diligence findings into a 15–20 page memo that will be scrutinised by senior partners and external IC members. A custom GPT trained on the firm's memo structure, tone and content expectations produces a credible first draft in minutes, freeing the analyst to focus on judgement rather than formatting.

**Famous Brands angle:**
The IC memo (`4a. Cowork_IC_Memo_off_2025_PDF.docx`) was co-authored with Claude in an earlier course module. Participants have already seen the output. Now they build the GPT that could produce that memo on demand — feeding in the extracted financials (`2a. 2025_extracted_details_Claude_CLI.md`) and the triage output (`2b. Triage_Output_From_md_Extract.md`) and watching a structured IC memo emerge.

**System prompt skeleton:**
```
You are a private equity investment analyst preparing Investment Committee memos
for [FIRM NAME].

When provided with financial extracts, triage analysis, or due diligence notes on
a target company, you draft a structured IC memo in the following format:

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1 paragraph: company, opportunity, recommendation)
2. BUSINESS OVERVIEW (3–5 bullets: what they do, how they make money, key metrics)
3. INVESTMENT THESIS (3 numbered points: why now, why us, why this asset)
4. FINANCIAL SUMMARY TABLE (Revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA margin, FCF, Net Debt/EBITDA
   — last 3 years + current year + 1-year forward estimate)
5. VALUE CREATION PLAN (3–5 specific initiatives with estimated impact where known)
6. KEY RISKS AND MITIGANTS (3 risks, each with a specific mitigant)
7. TRANSACTION OVERVIEW (proposed structure, entry multiple, return scenario —
   use placeholders if not provided)
8. RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS (numbered, owner-assigned, deadline-driven)

TONE: Precise, direct, and assertive. Avoid hedging language. Use active voice.
Write at the level of a Goldman Sachs or Actis analyst — not a consultant.

HOUSE STYLE RULES:
- No bullet points in the Executive Summary — prose only
- Financial figures in ZAR millions unless otherwise specified
- All growth rates are year-on-year unless otherwise stated
- Always include a "Key Risks" section even if input data is limited
- Flag clearly where assumptions have been made and what would change them

If critical information is missing, note it explicitly rather than inventing it.
Write placeholders in [SQUARE BRACKETS] for any data the analyst must fill in.
```

**Knowledge base:**
- Upload: `2a. 2025_extracted_details_Claude_CLI.md` (financial extract — the "raw material" the GPT converts)
- Upload: `2b. Triage_Output_From_md_Extract.md` (the triage analysis — the "analytical backbone" of the memo)
- Optionally upload: a sanitised prior IC memo as a style reference

**Demo output:**
Participants type: *"Draft an IC memo for Famous Brands using the uploaded extracts."* The GPT returns a structured 8-section memo with populated financials, a three-point investment thesis, value creation initiatives mapped to the Famous Brands case (supply chain efficiency, non-core disposals, digital/loyalty build-out), and bracketed placeholders for entry price and return scenario. The room immediately sees that 80% of the analyst's drafting work is done.

**Build time estimate:** 15–18 minutes (8 min to configure system prompt and house style rules, 5 min to upload the two knowledge files, 5 min to test and compare output to the co-authored memo from the earlier module)

### Option 3: Deal Red-Flag Reviewer

**One-line pitch:**
A GPT/Gem that reads any company document and returns an adversarial list of investment red flags, missing disclosures, and the questions an IC chair would ask — before the analyst walks into the room.

**PE workflow it replicates:**
Before every IC presentation, a deal team runs through what the IC chair will attack. The most experienced partners do this instinctively — they pattern-match against every deal they've ever seen blow up. A custom GPT trained on common PE red flags (governance, financial quality, leverage, management, sector dynamics) encodes that adversarial instinct and applies it systematically to any new document package.

**Famous Brands angle:**
The Famous Brands case contains real, textbook red flags: AME losing money at -60.5% margin, Nigeria capital fully impaired, UK revenue down 18%, franchisee health explicitly named as a top-10 group risk, a R450m debt maturity deferred, and Signature Brands loss-making. A vanilla ChatGPT read of the annual report will miss half of these or bury them in summaries. The red-flag GPT surfaces them in a structured, IC-ready format.

**Famous Brands angle (demo hook):**
Ask the plain GPT/Gemini: *"What are the risks in Famous Brands' FY2025 annual report?"* — it returns a generic list. Then ask the Red-Flag Reviewer the same question. It returns a structured adversarial brief with 8 specific flags, the evidence for each, and the IC question the flag would provoke. The contrast is immediately convincing.

**System prompt skeleton:**
```
You are a private equity Investment Committee chair reviewing a deal memo or
company document before a formal IC presentation.

Your job is NOT to support the deal — your job is to stress-test it. You are
looking for every reason this deal could go wrong, every gap in the analysis,
and every question the deal team has not answered.

When provided with any company document or investment memo, return a structured
RED FLAG BRIEF in the following format:

## RED FLAG BRIEF: [COMPANY NAME]

### 1. FINANCIAL QUALITY FLAGS
For each flag:
- Flag: [specific issue]
- Evidence: [quote or metric from the document]
- IC Question: [the question this would trigger in a real IC meeting]

### 2. BUSINESS MODEL FLAGS
[Same format]

### 3. GOVERNANCE & MANAGEMENT FLAGS
[Same format]

### 4. TRANSACTION STRUCTURE FLAGS
[Same format — leverage, price, deal terms, competitive dynamics]

### 5. MISSING DISCLOSURES
List everything critical that the document does NOT disclose but that a buyer
would need to underwrite this deal. Be specific.

### 6. TOP 5 QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT
The five hardest questions a senior IC member would ask management on this deal.

ADVERSARIAL RULES:
- Do not soften your flags. If something is a real risk, name it directly.
- Distinguish between risks that are deal-breakers and those that need mitigation.
- Do not invent risks — only flag what the document gives you evidence for.
- If the document is clean, say so and explain what that cleanliness is worth.
- Always complete the Missing Disclosures section, even for high-quality documents.
```

**Knowledge base:**
- Upload: `2b. Triage_Output_From_md_Extract.md` (so the GPT has the full Famous Brands analysis as context)
- No additional upload required — this GPT works powerfully with raw documents too
- Optionally upload: a firm-specific deal framework listing common sector red flags (e.g. QSR/consumer franchise red flags)

**Demo output:**
Participants upload the Famous Brands Integrated Report or paste the triage output. The GPT returns a 6-section Red Flag Brief covering: the AME impairment and Nigeria exit, deferral of the R450m debt facility, the absence of franchisee-level economics in any public disclosure, weak UK performance obscured by segment aggregation, the Signature Brands drag on group profitability, and five pointed IC questions including *"What is your franchisee renewal rate by brand, and what is your assessment of their ability to service debt at current food cost and rental levels?"* The room immediately recognises these as the questions a real IC chair would ask.

**Build time estimate:** 10–12 minutes (5 min to paste system prompt and read through adversarial rules, 3 min to upload the knowledge file, 4 min to test on Famous Brands material and discuss output with room)

## Facilitator Notes

**Sequencing suggestion:**
Run these exercises in order (1 → 2 → 3) if time permits, as they mirror the actual deal lifecycle: triage → memo → IC challenge. Each exercise builds on the same Famous Brands source material, which reinforces continuity across the module.

**Platform notes:**
- **ChatGPT (OpenAI):** Build these as Custom GPTs using the GPT Editor. System prompt goes in the "Instructions" field. Files upload to the knowledge base. Participants need a ChatGPT Plus or Team account.
- **Gemini (Google):** Build as Gems via the Gem editor in Gemini Advanced. System prompt goes in the persona/instructions field. File upload is supported via Google Drive or direct upload. Participants need a Gemini Advanced subscription.
- Both platforms handle these exercises equally well. The key differentiator is the system prompt quality, not the model.

**The "aha moment" to aim for:**
In each exercise, have a participant run the same query in a plain, unconfigured ChatGPT/Gemini tab alongside their configured GPT/Gem. The contrast — between a generic answer and a structured, firm-specific, evidence-grounded output — is the lesson. That is what a custom AI tool does: it encodes expertise into a repeatable workflow.
