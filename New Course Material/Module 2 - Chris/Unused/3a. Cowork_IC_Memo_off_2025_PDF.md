## II. Company Overview

### Business Description

Famous Brands is Africa's largest food services franchisor, tracing its origins to the Steers burger chain opened by George Halamandres in 1961. The Group listed on the JSE in November 1994 at a market cap of R41 million; it has since grown into a multi-brand, vertically integrated food services business with R8.3 billion in annual revenue (FY2025).

The business operates across four interconnected divisions:

**Brands** — 2,979 restaurants in 20 countries, of which 2,895 are franchised and 84 are Company-owned. The portfolio is segmented into *Leading Brands* (mainstream QSR and casual dining) and *Signature Brands* (niche, bespoke casual dining).

**Manufacturing** — 10 wholly or partly owned plants in Gauteng and Western Cape, producing meat, sauce, spice, coffee, ice cream, potato products, and cheese. Supplies the franchise network and generates standalone Retail revenue.

**Logistics** — 9 distribution centres, 96 trucks, and 3 cross-dock facilities supplying ingredients and products to restaurants across South Africa and select SADC markets.

**Retail** — 110 branded consumer products (sauces, frozen chips, coffee, meat) distributed to major South African retailers including Checkers, Pick n Pay, and Woolworths.

### Brand Portfolio

The Group manages 16 brands spanning the full value spectrum:

**Leading Brands — QSR:** Steers (671 restaurants, 13 markets), Debonairs Pizza (868 restaurants, 16 markets — the largest brand in the portfolio), Fishaways (242 restaurants), and Wimpy (UK: 61 restaurants; SA network reported within CDR).

**Leading Brands — CDR:** Wimpy SA, Mugg & Bean (generosity-led café brand, strong loyalty app), Milky Lane (soft-serve dessert concept).

**Signature Brands:** Mythos (Greek CDR), Turn 'n Tender (SA steakhouse), LUPA Osteria (Italian), SALSA (Mexican), PAUL (French bakery-café, master licence), Vovo Telo (artisan bakery), NetCafé and Coffee Couture (hospital captive channels), Fego Caffé (being converted to Mugg & Bean / Vovo Telo).

### Franchise Partner Dynamics

The franchise partner base is mature and diversified. The average tenure of SA franchise partners is 11 years, with several relationships exceeding 30 years. 42% of SA franchise partners operate a single restaurant; 24% operate two to five. Several partners have built multi-brand, multi-restaurant portfolios. Famous Brands reports a healthy pipeline of new franchise partner applications.

A geolocation analysis shows that 27% of Famous Brands' SA QSR network has no significant fast-food competitor within a 5km radius — a meaningful structural moat.

## III. Industry & Market

### Market Size and Dynamics

South Africa's food services industry is the Group's primary market. The QSR/food services sector has demonstrated structural resilience through cycles, underpinned by urbanisation, a growing lower-middle-income consumer base, and the ongoing shift from informal food preparation to branded restaurants.

Famous Brands occupies a dominant leadership position:
- Largest food services franchisor in Africa by restaurant count and revenue
- Portfolio spans every income and demographic band
- 30-year listed track record with no interruptions to dividend payments

### Secular Trends

**Positive tailwinds:** Drive-thru and delivery channels are growing rapidly, with consumers seeking convenience. Famous Brands has 58 drive-thrus in South Africa (targeting growth) and 51 delivery hubs serving 305 restaurants. The delivery hub model (one franchise partner fulfilling orders for multiple restaurants) differentiates Famous Brands from pure third-party aggregator dependency. Digital ordering and POS investment (Munch Software rollout across 268 CDRs) is modernising the consumer interface.

**Consumer technology:** App-enabled ordering, mobile-first consumer engagement, and data analytics represent a meaningful medium-term value creation opportunity. Famous Brands' investment in Munch Software (45.5% stake) provides POS integration and competitive advantage for franchise partners.

**Retail / at-home channel:** SA consumers increasingly seek branded food experiences at home. Famous Brands' 110-product Retail range provides diversified revenue and brand exposure beyond the restaurant footprint.

### Competitive Landscape

Famous Brands competes across QSR (McDonald's, KFC, Burger King, Chicken Licken), CDR (Spur Corporation, The Cooks' Table), and the grocery delivery / at-home dining space. Key competitive dynamics:

- The QSR delivery market is increasingly competitive, with major grocery retailers (Checkers Sixty60, Pick n Pay asap!) eroding food delivery market share
- Deep discounting by competitors has intensified during the weak consumer cycle
- Independent restaurants and food courts provide local competition in the CDR space
- Spur Corporation is Famous Brands' closest comparable in the SA listed franchise space

### Regulatory Environment

Famous Brands operates under the Companies Act (South Africa), the Consumer Protection Act, the Franchise Act, and labour legislation. The Group recently achieved a Level 1 B-BBEE rating (up from non-compliant in 2018), which is increasingly a prerequisite for commercial relationships in South Africa. B-BBEE compliance risk is flagged — the ownership pillar (27 points out of 110) is outside management's control and could cause the rating to fluctuate between Level 1 and Level 2.

## V. Investment Thesis

### Why Famous Brands is an Attractive Investment

**1. Dominant Market Position with High Barriers to Entry**

Famous Brands is Africa's largest food services franchisor with an unmatched brand portfolio across QSR and casual dining. The combination of 2,979 restaurants, 16 established brands, 30 years of franchise relationships, and a proprietary vertically integrated supply chain creates structural barriers to entry that would require years and substantial capital to replicate. No competitor in South Africa operates at comparable scale across multiple food categories.

**2. Asset-Light Franchisor Economics Generating Predictable, High-Quality Cash Flows**

The Group's core earnings engine — franchise royalties (typically 5–8% of restaurant turnover) and marketing fees — requires minimal capital deployment. As system-wide sales grow, royalty income grows proportionally with no incremental COGS. The franchise partner absorbs restaurant capex, operating risk, and labour costs. Famous Brands bears primarily head office and supply chain costs. This model generates structurally high cash conversion (100% cash realisation rate in FY2025) and ROCE of 34%.

**3. Vertically Integrated Supply Chain as a Sustainable Competitive Moat**

The Manufacturing and Logistics divisions are not merely cost centres — they are a strategic asset that provides franchise partners with price certainty, product consistency, supply chain resilience, and a structural cost advantage versus independent competitors. This integration deepens franchise partner dependency, improves network economics, and generates incremental revenue streams (Retail) from excess manufacturing capacity.

**4. Inflecting Financial Trajectory**

The financial profile is improving on multiple dimensions simultaneously: EBITDA margins are recovering (13.5% in FY2025 vs 12.9% in FY2023/24), leverage is declining rapidly (0.89x vs 1.14x in FY2023), ROCE is rising (34% vs 26%), and free cash flow is growing (+12% YoY). Management has reduced debt by R1.8bn since 2018. The business is at a leverage inflection point where capital allocation is shifting from debt reduction to network expansion and shareholder returns.

**5. Multiple Value Creation Levers Remain Underpenetrated**

Several organic value creation opportunities are not yet fully exploited: (a) drive-thru rollout (only 58 in SA, targeting acceleration), (b) delivery hub model (51 hubs serving 305 restaurants — substantial room to scale), (c) digital and data analytics capabilities (early stage), (d) Retail channel expansion with 110 products vs untapped category adjacencies, and (e) AME rationalisation to eliminate the R43m operating loss drag.

### Value Creation Plan and 100-Day Priorities

**Immediate (0–100 days):**
- Refinance R450m debt facility ahead of August 2026 maturity on terms aligned with private ownership
- Conduct detailed quality of earnings analysis — disaggregate royalty income from logistics pass-throughs
- Establish baseline for franchise partner profitability and identify stressed partners
- Review AME footprint and determine optimal exit/rationalisation path (8 countries, R43m operating loss)

**Medium-term (Year 1–3):**
- Accelerate SA Leading Brands network expansion (target 100+ net new stores per annum)
- Drive-thru rollout programme: target 150+ drive-thrus in SA within 3 years (from 58)
- Scale delivery hub model to 100+ hubs covering 500+ SA restaurants
- Complete Munch Software POS rollout across full CDR network; leverage data for personalised consumer engagement
- Optimise Supply Chain: complete Midrand cold storage commissioning and Manufacturing automation programme
- Selective non-core disposals: Wimpy UK strategic review; Fego Caffé conversion; minority associate rationalisation

**Long-term (Year 3–5):**
- AME profitable scaleback: focus on 3–4 high-potential markets (Botswana, Zambia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ethiopia) and exit others
- Retail revenue growth: expand branded product range; build direct-to-consumer e-commerce capability
- Explore bolt-on acquisition opportunities in adjacent food services categories

## X. Recommendation

**Proceed to detailed due diligence.**

Famous Brands is one of the most defensible franchise businesses in sub-Saharan Africa. Its portfolio of iconic brands, vertically integrated supply chain, improving financial trajectory, and declining leverage provide a compelling base for value creation. The asset-light core (royalty and fee income) generates strong cash flows, while identified value creation levers — network expansion, digital investment, delivery scale-up, and AME rationalisation — provide credible paths to EBITDA growth.

Returns are sensitive to entry price. At entry multiples below 10.0x EV/EBITDA, the deal generates attractive returns (>20% IRR) in the base case. At 11x or above, the IRR compresses significantly and the deal requires more aggressive assumptions on EBITDA growth or exit multiple.

**Conditions for advancement to IC approval:**
1. Completion of financial quality-of-earnings — particularly validation of normalised EBITDA and disaggregation of royalty vs. logistics revenue
2. Confirming viable B-BBEE ownership structure with an identified empowerment co-investor
3. Entry multiple agreed at or below 10.5x FY2025 EBITDA
4. Management retention and incentive plan terms aligned
5. No material undisclosed liabilities in AME or UK identified in legal DD
