# Comparison: Version 1 (PDF Triage) vs Version 2 (Markdown Extract Triage)
*Famous Brands — PE Screening Memo Comparison*
*Evaluated against prompt: 2c. Prompt_Compare_1c_2b.md*

---

## Overall Verdict

Version 2 (markdown extract) is the stronger screening document overall. It is more specific, more analytically disciplined, and more useful for a PE investor deciding whether to advance the opportunity. Crucially, it surfaces hard numbers — divisional margin detail, specific operating statistics, named brands — that Version 1 either omits or states at a higher level of generality. The gap is meaningful but not dramatic: Version 1 has good structure and is coherent throughout, but Version 2 pushes harder on the facts and is less prone to hedged generality.

The clearest difference is in analytical rigour: Version 2 distinguishes more explicitly between the quality of the core South African Leading Brands franchise engine and the drag from the wider group structure (Logistics, Signature Brands, AME, UK). That distinction — "good asset core wrapped in a more mixed group structure" — is the crux of the screening case, and Version 2 states it more clearly and earlier.

## Comparative Findings

### Version 1 Strengths

- Well-structured across all six screening stages with clear headers, which makes it easy for a reader to navigate.
- The "Provisional assessment" labels (Strong / Medium / High) at each stage are direct and decision-useful.
- Sponsorability section (Stage 2) identifies the key tension clearly: management already has the operational levers in play, so sponsor alpha is about speed and prioritisation rather than turnaround.
- Risk Concentration section is thorough and correctly clusters correlated risks (SA concentration + infrastructure + supply chain + franchisee health) into a coherent downside narrative.
- The Final Note ("quality listed compounder with some sponsorable traits") is an honest and apt summary assessment.
- Writing is fluent and appropriately cautious without being vague.

### Version 2 Strengths

- Provides significantly more specific financial and operational detail, including:
  - SA Leading Brands operating margin of 53.3%
  - Group operating margin 11.0% (up from 10.1%)
  - Logistics operating margin 1.4%; Retail 0.4%; Signature Brands -5.7%; AME -60.5%
  - UK margin down to 5.4%; UK revenue down 18%
  - Logistics is the largest revenue line at R5.358 billion vs Brands at only 14% of group revenue
  - 2,895 franchised stores vs 84 company-owned; 10 manufacturing plants, 9 logistics sites, 96 trucks
  - 27% of SA QSR network reportedly has no significant fast-food competitor within 5km
  - Specific portfolio actions: acquisition of remaining 38% of Famous Brands Coffee Company; disposal of 60% of Dial n Dine; Nigeria full capital impairment
- Business Quality assessment (Stage 1) is more honest: rates the overall business "Mixed" rather than "Strong", correctly reflecting the drag from non-core segments.
- Financial Conversion assessment (Stage 3) rates the business "Mixed financial conversion" rather than "Strong", which is more analytically defensible given the heavy logistics revenue base.
- The framing of "good asset core wrapped in a more mixed group structure" is more precise and underwriting-ready than Version 1's framing.
- Explicitly flags that Logistics is the largest revenue line and that this is not a clean royalty-light model — a key distinction that Version 1 acknowledges but does not emphasise as clearly.
- Stage 5 screening recommendation is more precise about what a sponsor would actually be underwriting (selective bet on Leading Brands + portfolio pruning) rather than treating the group as a single asset.
- Correctly notes at the end of Stage 5 that the materials do not support a decision to advance to full conviction underwriting — more precise than Version 1's "watchlist / selective further diligence."

### Version 1 Weaknesses

- Business Quality provisional rating of "Strong" overstates quality, given that Signature Brands are loss-making, AME margin is deeply negative (-60.5% per Version 2), and UK is under pressure. Version 1 acknowledges these but its headline rating is too generous.
- Financial Conversion provisional rating of "Strong financial conversion" similarly overstates quality given the logistics-heavy, low-margin revenue mix.
- Does not cite the SA Leading Brands operating margin of 53.3% or the AME operating margin of -60.5%, which are among the most decision-relevant numbers in the source material.
- Does not surface the Logistics revenue dominance (R5.358bn vs Brands at only 14% of group) — a structurally important point for understanding earnings quality.
- The 27% of SA QSR network with no significant competitor within 5km (a useful competitive moat indicator) is absent.
- Specific portfolio transactions (Coffee Company acquisition, Dial n Dine disposal, Nigeria impairment) are not named or used to support the sponsorability narrative.
- Slightly more prone to generalised language ("appears to have real scale", "appears strategically valuable") where Version 2 states the facts more directly.

### Version 2 Weaknesses

- The final line "Would you like this converted into a one-page investment memo, an IC-style summary, or a management presentation version?" is an artefact of conversational AI output and should not appear in a screening document. It undermines the professional presentation.
- The provisional assessment labels in Version 2 are embedded in the body text rather than called out in a consistent, scannable format — slightly harder to skim than Version 1's clearly labelled assessments.
- Business Snapshot section (Stage 0) is marginally less crisp in its opening framing than Version 1's clear "What we know / What is missing / Initial relevance" structure.
- Version 2 could be more explicit about the ROCE figure (34%), which is cited in Version 1 and is a useful capital efficiency indicator.

## Key Contradictions or Accuracy Concerns

**Between versions:**

- Business Quality rating: Version 1 says "Strong"; Version 2 says "Mixed". The Version 2 rating is better supported by the evidence, particularly given the loss-making Signature Brands and deeply negative AME margin.
- Financial Conversion rating: Version 1 says "Strong financial conversion"; Version 2 says "Mixed financial conversion". Again, Version 2's rating is more defensible given the logistics-heavy revenue structure.
- Both versions agree on risk concentration (High) and sponsorability (Medium), which suggests these are the more robustly assessed dimensions.

**Internal to Version 1:**

- Version 1 rates Business Quality as "Strong" but then says in the conclusion that it is "better described as a strong core business with some pockets of volatility." That hedging is inconsistent with the headline "Strong" rating.
- Version 1 rates Financial Conversion as "Strong" but then says "lower-margin supply chain activities and weaker geographies dilute earnings quality and could temper how aggressively a sponsor would lever the asset" — again, this qualifies the headline rating substantially.

**Internal to Version 2:**

- No material internal contradictions identified. The "Mixed" ratings in Stages 1 and 3 are well supported by the evidence cited.

**Factual accuracy:**

- Both versions are factually consistent on the key headline numbers (R8.283bn revenue, R914m operating profit, R1.121bn cash from operations, 0.89x net debt/EBITDA, R214m capex FY2025, R373m capex plan FY2026).
- Version 2's specific divisional margin figures appear directly drawn from the source material and are more granular than anything cited in Version 1.

## Final Recommendation

**Combine both, but anchor on Version 2**

- Version 2 is more factually specific, and in PE screening, specificity matters: divisional margins, operating statistics, and named portfolio actions are what allow an investor to form a real view.
- Version 2's quality assessments (Mixed for Business Quality and Financial Conversion) are more defensible than Version 1's overstated "Strong" ratings, which could mislead a senior investor into overweighting the group at the expense of scrutinising the non-core drag.
- Version 1 contributes better scannability (clearly labelled assessment boxes), a few facts Version 2 omits (ROCE), and tighter summary language in places — these should be grafted onto a Version 2 base.
- The combined output should remove the conversational artefact at the end of Version 2 and standardise the provisional assessment labels for ease of navigation.
