### Remuneration Policy (pages 137–148)

#### Philosophy

- Remuneration Policy applies to all employees and directors of Famous Brands (not to associates and franchise partners)
- Designed around themes: support for business strategy; maintain a competitive reward system; apply consistent and responsible remuneration practices; develop performance metrics that are demanding and sustainable
- Policy also considers King IV (principle 14), the Companies Act 2008, the Basic Conditions of Employment Act 1997, Employment Equity Act 1998, and other applicable legislation

#### Key principles

- Reward, retain, and where necessary, attract talent through fair, transparent and competitive remuneration
- Reward short-term and long-term performance by linking STIs to operational, financial and other targets; and LTIs to the achievement of Famous Brands' strategic objectives
- Key focus areas are reflected in the scorecards of executive management and the annual performance evaluations for employees; scorecards reflect key performance areas (KPAs) and associated KPIs
- We reward for value created, contribution and performance to ensure alignment with shareholder interests, balancing this across economic, social, and environmental aspects
- Employee rewards are influenced by individual and Company performance, and employees' contributions are recognised through a discretionary performance bonus

#### Fair and responsible remuneration principles

- Remuneration must be free from any form of discrimination
- Market benchmarking refers to the correct remuneration bands and levels with progression reflected for experience and accountability
- Remuneration design and application must drive internal and external parity
- All remuneration components are designed and implemented in accordance with applicable tax and regulatory requirements
- Performance and value are defined and measured over the short, medium and long terms and protect shareholders' interests
- An overarching commitment to ethical and legally defensible remuneration practices

#### Benchmark methodology

- Famous Brands undertakes a total remuneration benchmark at least every two years, using reputable remuneration consultants
- Benchmark 1: uses a database covering over 700 South African companies; extracts data from companies and creates a theoretical job match based on the scope of the role; sized relative to the actual CEO role
- Benchmark 2: from a specified comparator group of companies approved by the Committee (see page 144)

#### Equal pay and gender equality

- Conducts an equal pay for equal value of work audit as part of the annual salary increase exercise in March each year
- Track equal pay across both genders as a KPI within ESG Framework
- In 2025, salary adjustments applied to: 9 African females, 4 African males, 1 White female, 1 Indian female

#### Malus and clawback (page 146)

- Famous Brands has malus and clawback provisions that enable adjustments to variable pay
- The Board may act on recommendation of the Committee to reduce/cancel/adjust unvested variable remuneration (malus) or to recover vested/paid variable remuneration (clawback) where there is reasonable evidence of:
  - Personally acting fraudulently or dishonestly or in a manner that adversely affects the Company's reputation or is characterised as gross misconduct
  - Directing an employee, contractor or adviser to act fraudulently, dishonestly, or to undertake other misconduct
  - Receiving an STI or LTI award because of fraud, dishonesty or a breach of obligation committed by another person
  - Receiving an STI or LTI award because of an intentional error in calculating a performance measure

#### Recruitment, contracts and termination (page 147)

- Comparative benchmarking is done to determine the size, nature and complexity of the role and the availability of skills before making a competitive offer
- The intention is not to grant more than what the appointee would have received from the Company in a 12-month period
- All executive team members have permanent employment contracts (open-ended)
- Executive management team has a three-month notice period

#### Minimum shareholding requirements (page 148)

- Executive directors are required to build and maintain a minimum holding of Famous Brands shares

#### Non-executive directors' fees (page 148)

- Non-executive directors receive formal letters of appointment and are compensated in accordance with their role
- Directors receive a flat fee and are not paid for meeting attendance
- Fees are reviewed annually and increases are implemented in June following approval by shareholders at the AGM
- The level of fees is set using a comparable benchmark group derived from companies with similar size, complexity, and geographic spread
- Non-executive directors are not eligible to receive any short or long-term incentives
