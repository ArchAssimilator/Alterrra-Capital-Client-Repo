Slide 1
Title: Famous Brands 2025: A scaled franchising platform with integrated control
Subtitle: Africa’s leading food services franchisor enters 2025 from a position of scale and operational breadth

* Famous Brands operates a vertically integrated model across four pillars: Brands, Manufacturing, Logistics and Retail.
* The group has 16 restaurant brands, 2,979 restaurants across 20 countries, and 4,522 employees.
* The network is predominantly franchised: 2,895 franchised restaurants and 84 company-owned sites.
* The business has been listed on the JSE for 30 years and positions itself as a responsible franchisor with a mature governance environment.
* Franchise partners are central to the model, with 1,055 partners and several operating multi-brand, multi-restaurant networks.

Subtitle: Brand royalties are supported by an in-house supply chain that strengthens consistency and economics

* Famous Brands earns sales-based royalty income from franchise partners and provides full business format franchising support.
* Its supply chain supports franchise partners through efficient supply, price certainty, product innovation and margin management.
* The logistics footprint includes six distribution centres, three cross-dock facilities, a fleet of 96 trucks, and support from 33 owner-driver businesses.
* Retail products span 110 SKUs and reach 2,893 retail outlets, extending the group beyond restaurant channels.
* South Africa is the anchor market, with SADC, AME and the UK providing additional growth and diversification exposure.

Slide 3

Title: 2025 opened in a difficult consumer environment
Subtitle: Macro improvement did not translate into the uplift in spending the group expected

* The first half was weighed down by pre-election uncertainty and poor economic conditions, with South African GDP growth of 0.5% in the first half of calendar 2024.
* The second half brought better confidence signals: formation of the GNU, progress on load shedding, the two-pot retirement system and initial interest rate cuts.
* Food inflation moderated into the 3% to 6% target range, but that tailwind still did not produce the expected improvement in consumer spending.
* Tourism in South Africa remained subdued, and weaker front-end brand demand flowed through into Manufacturing and Logistics.
* Several markets outside South Africa also faced inflation, political turbulence and economic pressure.

Slide 4
Title: Despite pressure, the group still delivered growth and margin resilience
Subtitle: Revenue, profit and cash generation all improved in 2025

* Revenue increased 3.2% to R8.283 billion, supported by Leading Brands in South Africa and inflationary price increases.
* Operating profit rose 12.6% to R914 million, with group operating margin improving to 11.0% from 10.1%.
* Cash generated from operations increased to R1.121 billion, reflecting a cash realisation rate of 100%.
* Net debt to EBITDA improved to 0.89x, giving the business more balance sheet flexibility.
* Management credits cost containment, supply chain efficiencies and reduced load shedding relief for protecting profitability.

Slide 5

Title: South Africa remains the engine of the business
Subtitle: Scale and profitability are concentrated in the home market

* South Africa accounted for 64% of segment revenue, 43% operating profit margin in the Brands view, and 2,626 restaurants at year-end.
* SADC contributed 25% of segment revenue and 224 restaurants, showing meaningful regional scale but tougher operating conditions in several countries.
* The UK and AME remain much smaller in footprint and economics, with the UK at 61 restaurants and AME at 68.
* The UK had an especially difficult year, with revenue down 18% and operating margin down to 5.4%.
* AME remained challenging, with a negative operating margin and disruption from exited markets.

Slide 6

Title: Vertical integration helped absorb external shocks
Subtitle: Manufacturing improved materially even as Logistics and Retail stayed thin margin

* Manufacturing revenue increased to R3.371 billion and operating profit rose to R371 million, lifting margin to 11.0% from 9.0%.
* Management attributes the improvement to better production yield, output gains, lower diesel costs and cost-saving initiatives.
* Logistics generated R5.226 billion of segment revenue but margin remained thin at 1.4%, affected by product mix and system implementation costs.
* Retail revenue declined to R344 million and operating margin fell to 0.4%, pressured by lower frozen potato chip volumes and renewed competitor activity.
* The takeaway: the integrated platform provides resilience, but not all parts of the model convert equally into profit.

Slide 7

Title: Cash generation remains a core strength
Subtitle: Operating cash flow funded capex, dividends and debt reduction

* The group generated R1.1 billion in cash from operations and free cash flow of R753 million in 2025.
* Internally generated cash funded R345 million of dividends, R214 million of capex and R160 million of debt repayments.
* Liquidity headroom improved to R674 million, made up of cash balances and undrawn committed facilities.
* Net working capital rose to R399 million, but management still describes the group as highly cash generative.
* Capex continues to support long-term capacity, including consumer-facing technology and the Midrand cold storage facility.

Slide 8

Title: Resolution: invest for resilience while managing concentrated risks
Subtitle: The business enters 2026 with strong cash flow, but exposure remains tightly linked to South Africa and franchise partner health

* Famous Brands identifies unreliable electricity supply and franchise partner profitability as major risks to restaurants and the supply chain.
* Management’s response includes backup power, smarter equipment, hybrid energy exploration, lease support and relief packages for franchise partners.
* 2026 priorities include expanding high-potential sites, strengthening drive-thru and delivery capabilities, and rolling out more consumer-facing technology.
* The year’s core message is one of controlled progress: modest top-line growth, stronger profitability, disciplined capital allocation and ongoing investment in future capability.
* In Gamma terms, the story ends with a business that is not immune to pressure, but is using scale, integration and cash generation to defend and extend its position.
