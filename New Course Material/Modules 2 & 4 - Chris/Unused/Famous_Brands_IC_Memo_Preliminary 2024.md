# Preliminary Investment Committee Memo
## Famous Brands Limited (JSE: FBR)
**Date:** March 2026 | **Classification:** Confidential | **Status:** First-Pass Deal Screen

---

## I. Executive Summary

Famous Brands Limited is Africa's largest food services franchisor, operating a network of 2,979 restaurants across 20 countries under 16 iconic brands including Debonairs Pizza, Steers, Wimpy, and Mugg & Bean. The Group is vertically integrated across Brands, Manufacturing, Logistics, and Retail, with a deeply entrenched position in the South African QSR/CDR market.

The business delivered a solid FY2025 result in a difficult consumer environment: revenue grew 3.2% to R8.3bn, operating profit rose 12.6% to R914m, and leverage fell to 0.89x net debt/EBITDA — the lowest in nearly a decade following R1.8bn of debt reduction since 2018. HEPS reached 520 cents, up 11.8% year-on-year.

**Headline investment considerations:**

- High-quality, capital-light franchise model with a 34% ROCE and recurring royalty/supply chain cash flows
- Dominant domestic market position (88% of network in SA); meaningful barriers to entry
- Prolonged SA consumer pressure has driven like-for-like growth to 1.3%, raising questions about medium-term organic growth headroom
- International expansion (SADC, AME, UK) is subscale and currently loss-making or marginally profitable
- Leverage is improving and the balance sheet is manageable, but refinancing of R450m deferred to August 2026 is a near-term watch item

**Preliminary recommendation:** Proceed to detailed diligence. The combination of a franchise-led model with high returns, a clean-up trajectory on the balance sheet, and suppressed like-for-like trends (which may represent a buying opportunity) warrants deeper work before a definitive position can be formed.

## II. Company Overview

**Business description:** Famous Brands is a full-service franchise system. The Group earns royalties and franchise fees from its brand portfolio and captures a second margin layer through a vertically integrated supply chain — manufacturing and distributing food and packaging inputs directly to franchisees. This "double dip" model (franchise margin + supply chain margin) differentiates it from pure-play franchisors and underpins its cash generation profile.

## IV. Investment Thesis

**1. Structural market position with high barriers to entry.** Famous Brands controls South Africa's largest QSR/CDR franchise network with 2,626 restaurants, 27% of which have no significant fast-food competitor within a 5km radius. Brand equity is deep: 21 consumer awards in 2025, average franchise partner tenure of 11 years, and a loyalty programme with 941,000 members.

**2. Capital-light, high-returns franchise model.** The royalty-plus-supply-chain model generates consistently high returns (ROCE: 34%) with low incremental capital requirements. 72% of new restaurant openings go to existing franchise partners, reducing franchise development risk and reinforcing retention. Cash conversion is strong — cash from operations of R1.1bn closely tracks EBITDA.

**3. Balance sheet repair creating optionality.** Management has reduced debt by R1.8bn since 2018. Net leverage is at a multi-year low of 0.89x. This creates room for capital returns, M&A, or a PE acquirer to apply moderate leverage without immediately stressing covenants.

**4. Operational self-help levers not yet fully realised.** Manufacturing margins expanded 200bps in FY2025 and the new Midrand cold storage facility (opened June 2025) should generate further logistics cost savings. A technology rollout (Munch POS, digital menus, self-service terminals) is underway but early-stage. Drive-thru expansion and own-delivery build-out represent identifiable near-term earnings drivers.

**5. Potential valuation entry point on cyclical weakness.** SA like-for-like growth has decelerated sharply: 13.5% in FY2023, 4.1% in FY2024, 1.3% in FY2025 — reflecting a prolonged SA consumer downcycle. If the macro improves and LFL recovers to mid-single digits, earnings leverage through the supply chain model could be material. An acquirer entering at trough LFL multiples would benefit disproportionately.

## VI. Preliminary Recommendation

**Recommendation: Proceed to Phase II diligence — conditional**

Famous Brands is a high-quality franchise asset with a dominant domestic market position, strong cash generation, and an improving balance sheet. The risk-adjusted profile warrants deeper work, particularly around:

1. **Valuation.** Current JSE market capitalisation and implied EV/EBITDA multiple need to be assessed against comparable franchise businesses (Recipe Group, Spur, global comps such as Yum! Brands). At what entry multiple does the thesis work?

2. **LFL recovery scenario analysis.** Model the earnings impact if SA LFL recovers to 3–5% over 2–3 years, and sensitise to the downside case where consumer pressure persists. This is the key swing factor in the return profile.

3. **Debt structure and refinancing terms.** Understand the terms and likely outcome of the August 2026 refinancing. Assess any covenant package and change-of-control provisions.

4. **International losses.** Quantify the earnings drag from AME and UK, and assess whether the Group would be worth more with a simplified, SA-focused structure.

5. **Management alignment.** If a take-private, assess CEO/CFO retention, LTI terms, and Board succession pipeline (several long-standing Board members are under succession review).

**Next steps:**
- Pull JSE market data and construct EV/EBITDA and P/HEPS multiple framework
- Commission management meeting / preliminary commercial DD
- Review full AFS (KPMG-audited, FY2025)
- Map comparable transaction multiples (SA and EM franchise assets)
