## Example 1: Investment Due Diligence

**User input:** "Research Shoprite Holdings as a potential investment"

**Generated prompt:**

```markdown
## Research Brief: Shoprite Holdings Investment Due Diligence

### Context & Motivation
Evaluating Shoprite Holdings (SHP) as a potential equity investment for a South African-focused portfolio. This research must provide a comprehensive, evidence-based assessment of the company's competitive position, growth trajectory, and risk profile to inform a buy/hold/avoid decision.

### Primary Research Question
Is Shoprite Holdings positioned to deliver above-market returns over the next 3-5 years, and what are the key risks that could derail this thesis?

### Sub-Questions to Investigate
1. **Competitive position**: How does Shoprite's market share, store economics, and operational efficiency compare to Pick n Pay, Woolworths Food, Spar, and Massmart? What is the trajectory?
2. **Growth engines**: What are the realistic growth vectors — new store rollout, format innovation (e.g., Checkers Sixty60), African expansion, fintech (Money Market Account)? What is the TAM for each?
3. **Financial health**: Analyse 5-year trends in revenue growth, margin progression, ROIC, free cash flow generation, and balance sheet strength. How does capex intensity compare to peers?
4. **Management quality**: Assess the Pieter Engelbrecht leadership era — strategic decisions, capital allocation track record, corporate governance quality
5. **Risk factors**: Load-shedding impact and diesel costs, food inflation trajectory, competitive response from Pick n Pay's turnaround plan, regulatory risks, African expansion country risk

### Analytical Framework
Apply a combination of:
- Porter's Five Forces for industry structural analysis
- DuPont decomposition for return-on-equity drivers
- Scenario analysis (bull/base/bear) for 3-year forward valuation

### Source & Evidence Requirements
- Cross-reference annual reports, sell-side analyst research, StatsSA data, competition commission filings, and business journalism (Business Day, Financial Mail, Daily Maverick)
- Distinguish between company-reported metrics and independently verified data
- Flag any data points older than 12 months

### Confidence & Uncertainty Protocol
- Rate each finding: High / Medium / Low confidence
- Distinguish between: established fact, emerging consensus, informed speculation, unknown
- Explicitly note where bull and bear arguments diverge

### Output Structure
1. **Executive Summary** (300 words) — investment thesis with key risks
2. **Competitive Analysis** — with evidence and confidence ratings
3. **Financial Deep-Dive** — ratio analysis and trend assessment
4. **Growth Opportunity Assessment** — TAM, execution risk, timeline
5. **Risk Matrix** — probability and impact assessment
6. **Synthesis** — bull/base/bear scenarios with implied valuation ranges
7. **Knowledge Gaps** — what couldn't be determined
8. **Recommended Next Steps** — specific follow-up due diligence

### Quality Criteria
- Prioritise contrarian insights over consensus repetition
- Identify the 3-5 things a generalist investor would most likely miss
- Think step by step through each dimension before synthesising
```

## Example 2: Market Entry Research

**User input:** "Research the opportunity for expanding into the East African renewable energy market"

**Generated prompt:**

```markdown
## Research Brief: East African Renewable Energy Market Entry Assessment

### Context & Motivation
A South African infrastructure fund is evaluating market entry into East African renewable energy. This research must assess market attractiveness, entry barriers, competitive dynamics, and optimal entry strategy to support a go/no-go investment committee decision.

### Primary Research Question
What is the risk-adjusted attractiveness of the East African renewable energy market for a South African infrastructure investor, and what would be the optimal entry strategy?

### Sub-Questions to Investigate
1. **Market sizing and trajectory**: What is the current installed capacity, pipeline, and 5-year growth forecast for solar, wind, geothermal, and hydro across Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, and Ethiopia?
2. **Policy and regulatory environment**: What are the IPP frameworks, feed-in tariffs, auction mechanisms, and political stability factors in each target market? How do they compare to SA's REIPPP?
3. **Competitive landscape**: Who are the active developers, DFIs, and institutional investors? What are typical project returns, and how have they trended?
4. **Operational risks**: Grid infrastructure reliability, currency risk (KES, TZS, UGX, RWF), land acquisition complexity, permitting timelines, force majeure considerations
5. **Entry strategy options**: Greenfield development vs. acquisition of operating assets vs. co-investment with established developers — pros/cons of each for a new entrant

### Analytical Framework
- PESTEL analysis for each target country
- Comparative market attractiveness scoring matrix
- Risk-return scatter plot framework

### Source & Evidence Requirements
- Cross-reference IEA/IRENA data, World Bank Doing Business indicators, national energy ministry publications, AFDB project databases, and specialist publications (African Energy, ESI Africa)
- Distinguish between announced pipeline (aspirational) and financially closed projects (committed)
- Flag where data availability differs significantly between countries

### Confidence & Uncertainty Protocol
- Rate each country assessment: High / Medium / Low confidence
- Distinguish between: established fact, policy intention vs. implementation reality, informed speculation
- Where country risk assessments diverge between sources, present the range

### Output Structure
1. **Executive Summary** (300 words) — headline assessment with recommended priority markets
2. **Country-by-Country Analysis** — structured consistently for comparability
3. **Comparative Scoring Matrix** — multi-dimensional market attractiveness
4. **Entry Strategy Recommendation** — with supporting rationale and key assumptions
5. **Risk Register** — top 10 risks with mitigation strategies
6. **Knowledge Gaps** — data limitations and recommended primary research
7. **Next Steps** — specific actions for investment committee

### Quality Criteria
- Distinguish between de jure regulatory frameworks and de facto implementation reality
- Identify the 3-5 things a South African investor without East African experience would most likely underestimate
- Think step by step through each country before synthesising the cross-market comparison
```

## Example 3: Technology Landscape

**User input:** "Research how LLMs are being used in compliance and regulatory reporting"

**Generated prompt:**

```markdown
## Research Brief: LLM Applications in Compliance and Regulatory Reporting

### Context & Motivation
A financial services firm is evaluating whether to build, buy, or partner for LLM-powered compliance solutions. This research must map the current landscape of LLM applications in compliance, assess maturity levels, and identify the most promising near-term use cases to inform a technology strategy decision.

### Primary Research Question
What is the current state of LLM adoption in financial compliance and regulatory reporting, and which use cases have progressed beyond pilot to deliver measurable ROI?

### Sub-Questions to Investigate
1. **Use case taxonomy**: What are the distinct categories of LLM application in compliance (e.g., regulatory change management, suspicious activity narrative generation, policy gap analysis, regulatory reporting automation)?
2. **Maturity assessment**: For each use case, what is the maturity level — research/concept, pilot, production at scale? Who are the leading vendors and early adopters?
3. **Technical architecture**: What are the dominant architectural patterns (RAG, fine-tuning, agent-based)? What are the implications for data privacy, model governance, and auditability?
4. **Regulatory posture**: How are regulators (SEC, FCA, SARB, ECB) viewing LLM use in compliance? What guidance has been issued? What are the emerging risks around model risk management?
5. **ROI evidence**: Where have firms published or disclosed measurable outcomes from LLM compliance tools? What are the realistic efficiency gains vs. vendor claims?

### Analytical Framework
Apply a technology maturity matrix (experimental → pilot → production → scaled) crossed with business value (efficiency gain, risk reduction, regulatory requirement).

### Source & Evidence Requirements
- Cross-reference academic papers, regtech vendor publications, regulatory guidance documents, consulting firm reports (McKinsey, Deloitte, EY), and specialist compliance publications
- Distinguish between vendor marketing claims and independently verified outcomes
- Prioritise evidence from 2024-2026; flag pre-2024 findings as potentially outdated given the pace of LLM development

### Confidence & Uncertainty Protocol
- Rate each use case maturity assessment: High / Medium / Low confidence
- Distinguish between: demonstrated in production, successful pilot, theoretical/claimed, speculative
- Flag where the evidence base is dominated by vendor-produced content

### Output Structure
1. **Executive Summary** (300 words)
2. **Use Case Landscape** — taxonomy with maturity and evidence ratings
3. **Vendor & Solution Mapping** — key players by use case
4. **Regulatory Environment** — current guidance and emerging risks
5. **Build vs. Buy Analysis** — decision framework for each use case category
6. **Knowledge Gaps** — where evidence is thin
7. **Recommended Next Steps** — specific evaluation actions

### Quality Criteria
- Be sceptical of vendor claims — distinguish marketing from evidence
- Identify the 3-5 things a compliance executive without AI expertise would most likely misunderstand about LLM capabilities and limitations
- Think step by step through each use case before synthesising the landscape view
```

## Quick Reference: Prompt Structure Cheat Sheet

| Section | Purpose | Claude Strength It Activates |
|---|---|---|
| Context & Motivation | Anchors the research in a real decision | Ensures relevance and practical focus |
| Primary Research Question | Single, scoped question | Prevents scope creep in extended thinking |
| Sub-Questions | 4-6 analytical dimensions | Activates multi-step reasoning chains |
| Analytical Framework | Named framework(s) to apply | Claude applies frameworks rigorously, not superficially |
| Source & Evidence Requirements | Cross-referencing instructions | Triggers source triangulation behaviour |
| Confidence & Uncertainty Protocol | Explicit calibration instructions | Activates Claude's calibrated uncertainty |
| Output Structure | Numbered sections with word limits | Maintains quality throughout long outputs |
| Quality Criteria | Meta-instructions for depth | Triggers non-obvious insight generation |
